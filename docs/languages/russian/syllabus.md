# The Russian course: syllabus (scope and sequence)

*Owner: russian-language-teacher. Draft 2026-11-03, for the owner's OK. Plan only; nothing here is built.*
*Evidence and sources: `docs/research/russian-course-2026-11-03.md` (keys in square brackets below, for example [ZHU]). Shared frame: `docs/languages/README.md`, `docs/curriculum.md` (mastery, hints, feedback, placement), `docs/PLAN.md` › Learners & age bands.*

**Conventions.** Stress is marked with U+0301 in this file (рука́) for the voice and the reviewers; it is not shown to the child except where a skill teaches it (B-17, C-10, C-21, and owner question 6). ё is written wherever it belongs and never carries a stress mark (it is always stressed). Russian text is left to right, in an isolated run inside the right-to-left app. Sounds are written in square brackets in the Russian school way: [м] hard, [м’] soft.

## 1. What the course is

A course in Russian for **any** child aged 3 to 8 whose app language is Hebrew: listening, speaking, **reading and writing in Cyrillic**, from the first sound to reading and writing short texts. Lessons are taught in Hebrew by a voice that speaks Hebrew and Russian (owner's decision 6); everything the child is learning is heard in Russian.

|---|---|---|---|

## 2. The method in one page
1. **Meaning first.** New words come with a picture and an action, in short repeated routines, in Russian only (no Hebrew translation of the word in the voice; the picture is the meaning) [ASH][MOL][MUN].
2. **Sounds before letters** (the Elkonin–Zhurova sound-analytic method) [ZHU][NRP][RAK]: the child stretches each sound of a word, lays chips in a row of boxes (one per sound), then colours them: **red vowel, blue hard consonant, green soft consonant**; finds the stressed vowel; counts syllables by vowels.
3. **Letters for sounds**, a few at a time (§3), always both ways: hear a sound → find its letter; see a letter → say or choose its sound. Letter **names** wait until Band C (C-25); the child learns sounds.
4. **Reading by the syllable merge** (слияние): the consonant "runs to" the vowel and they are said together (ммма → ма). The **vowel strip** in pairs (а–я, о–ё, у–ю, ы–и, э–е) teaches that the vowel letter tells whether the consonant before it is hard or soft [ZHU].
5. **Decodable words only**: a word appears for reading only when every letter in it has been taught (§4), and, for new learners, only when the child knows the word's meaning.
6. **Write to learn letters**: trace with a guide, then a start dot only, then **write from memory** [JAM][LON]; spell with **letter tiles** (the cut-out alphabet) before handwriting can [ZHU].
7. **Retrieval and spacing**: every session opens with earlier skills; revisits on the app's schedule (`docs/curriculum.md` §5.7, §10).
8. **Three tries, hints that change the help, never "wrong"** (`docs/curriculum.md` §9.2). An unclear spoken answer is never counted.

## 3. Letter order (33 letters in 10 groups)

| Group | Letters | Why here | First syllables | Band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | А У О М | Three clear vowels that exist in Hebrew; М can be stretched, so the first merge is easy to hear (ммма). Gives the first word, ма́ма. | ма мо му, ам ум ом | B |
| 2 | С Н Ы | Two frequent, stretchable consonants (с, н: 5th and 7th most frequent [NKRY]); ы early because it is new to Hebrew ears and needed with hard consonants (мы, сын). | са со су сы, на но ну ны | B |
| 3 | Л Р И | Frequent sonorants; И brings the **first soft consonants** (ли, ми, ни, си, ри) and the contrast with ы (мы / ми, лы / ли) while the hard row is fresh. И is separated from Н by one group (look-alike for children who know Latin N/H). | ла ло лу лы ли, ра … ри | B |
| 4 | К Т В | Frequent; К and Т are stops, met after the child can merge with stretchable sounds. В is the 9th most frequent letter. | ка ко ку, та то ту ты ти, ва во ву вы ви | B |
| 5 | Е П З | Е is the 2nd most frequent letter [NKRY]; it softens the consonant (лес, мел) and says [йэ] at the start of a word (ель). П is kept away from Н and И (print look-alikes). З after С (pair [с]/[з]). | пе, ле, ме, не, зе; па по пу пы пи | C (B for fast learners) |
| 6 | Д Г Я Ь | Д is three groups after В (shape confusion б/в/д kept apart). Я: its two jobs (я́ма, мя́со — taught with the vowel strip а–я). Ь: softness at the end of a word (конь, соль, пять). | да ду ды ди де, га го гу, ля ня мя ся тя | C |
| 7 | Б Ч Ш Й | Б two groups after В. Ч is always soft; ча, чу are written with а, у (spelling rule later). Ш is always hard; separated from Щ (group 10). Й four groups after И. | ба бо бу бы би, ча чу чи, ша шо шу ши, ай ой уй | C |
| 8 | Ж Ё Ю | Ж pairs with Ш (жи–ши); Ё always stressed (ёж, ёлка, мёд); Ю completes the pairs у–ю. Ж and Х (look-alike) are kept in different groups. | жа жо жу жи, лё нё, лю ню | C |
| 9 | Х Ц Э | Less frequent. Ц is always hard (цирк, огуре́ц); Э mostly at the start of a word (э́то, э́хо). | ха хо ху хи, ца цо цу цы | C |
| 10 | Щ Ф Ъ | The least frequent letters [NKRY]. Щ is always soft (щу́ка, щи); Ф (фото, флаг); Ъ separates (съел ↔ сел). | ща щу щи, фа фо фу | C |

Notes:
- The first 15 letters by corpus frequency (о е а и н т с р в л к м д п у) are all in groups 1–6 [NKRY].
- Capital and small forms are taught together (Аа). Most Cyrillic small letters are smaller copies of the capitals (к, м, н, т, п, ш), so the load is lighter than in Latin; the ones that differ (а, б, е, р, у) get their own matching items.
- Look-alike pairs taught in different groups: Н/И/П (1 group apart at least), Б/В/Д, Ш/Щ, И/Й, Ж/Х, З/Э.
- Sound pairs voiced/voiceless (б–п, в–ф, г–к, д–т, ж–ш, з–с) are compared once both letters are known (C-20).
- This is a hybrid of the Goretsky order (vowels first, then by frequency) and the Zhukova order (stretchable М, С first) [GOR]; no trial compares orders (research report §4.1).

## 6. Grammar met as chunks
Grammar is never explained to Band A–B; it is heard and used in fixed phrases with pictures. Band C names a few rules only where spelling needs them.

| Где …? — Вот … | Где мяч? — Вот мяч. | A-13 | |
| Дай, пожа́луйста …; Я хочу́ … | Дай, пожа́луйста, мяч. Я хочу́ сок. | A-14 | accusative met unnoticed (мяч, сок are the same as the nominative) |

| Questions | Кто? Что? Где? Когда́? Почему́? | C-27 | |
| Number + noun | оди́н кот, два кота́, пять кото́в | C-28 | |

Voice rule (as for Hebrew, `docs/PLAN.md` › Speaking to the child): core lines never reveal the speaker's gender, so the teaching voice avoids the past tense about itself (я был / я была́) and adjectives about itself; present tense and imperatives are gender-neutral in Russian. Instructions address the child with ты in the Russian clips only where a Russian line is needed (Band C reading prompts); the Hebrew instruction carries the rest.

### 7.3 The placement game
- **Parent questionnaire** (parent area, enums only, nothing free-text, nothing sent to a vendor): Russian at home (no one / one parent / both / grandparents or others); the child understands (no / some / most); speaks (no / words / sentences); knows Russian letters (no / some / most); reads Russian (no / syllables / words / sentences). It chooses where each staircase **starts**, nothing more.
- **Five short staircases**, 4–8 items each, the app's rule (two right → up a level; one wrong → down; stop after 2 reversals or 8 items; place one unit below the highest level with ≥ 2/3 right) [GAG10 for what to sample]:
  1. Understanding: hear a word → tap its picture (4 pictures) → a chunk (Где мяч?) → a sentence (Ко́шка спит на сту́ле) → a grammar contrast (Я ви́жу ко́шку / ко́шка ви́дит меня́, picture choice).
  2. Sounds: rhyme → syllable count → first sound → hard or soft.
  3. Letters: hear a sound → tap the letter, rising through the groups.
  4. Reading: syllable → word → sentence, each matched to a picture.
  5. Spelling: build a heard word from tiles.
- Every placement item gets positive feedback; nothing is shown to the child; the parent sees "we start here" per strand and can move it one level. Speaking is not placed (no Russian recogniser); the parent's answer sets it.

## 8. Speaking without a Russian recogniser
The app's recogniser is Hebrew only. Until a Russian on-device recogniser is approved and tested (owner question 4), speaking skills are **practised and modelled, never scored**: the voice says the word, the child is invited to say it too (for example "נַגִּיד יַחַד", a draft marked `"review": "needs-editor"` for the hebrew-text-editor), a pause, the voice says it again; the child or a parent may tap "I said it" (counts as practice, not as mastery evidence beyond self-report). No audio is recorded or played back. SPK skills reach mastery by the parent's confirmation in the parent area (a weekly "did you hear …?" with the clip), or stay "practised".

## 9. Mastery
Every skill uses the app's standard evidence (`docs/curriculum.md` §4.1 SE: P(L) ≥ 0.95; 4 independent correct over ≥ 2 days, 6 for core skills; ≥ 2 representations; ≥ 1 understanding check; durable mastery after a delayed check ≥ 7 days later). The "Mastery" column adds what the representations must be. Core skills (marked ●) need 6 correct and 2 checks. 2-choice items count at most half of the evidence.

## 12. What is out of scope, and why
- **Cursive** (прописи): not in this plan (research report §3.5; owner question 3).
- **Explicit case tables, verb aspect, verbs of motion as a system**: too abstract for 3–8; met only as chunks.
- **Scoring speech**: no Russian recogniser; children's recognition is unreliable (`docs/curriculum.md` principle 9).
- **Russian culture, history or holidays beyond everyday child life and folk tales**: the role's boundary.
- **Translation exercises** Hebrew ↔ Russian: meaning by pictures instead.
