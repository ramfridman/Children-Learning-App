## 1. Goal and "done when"

From PLAN.md, unchanged:

- The on-device speech model, the answer matcher and the mic states.
- **Done when:** accepted answers are right at least 97% of the time on a family recording set; unclear speech is never marked wrong.

M5 lets a child answer by talking. The child taps the mic, says a word ("שָׁלוֹשׁ", "אָדֹם"), the phone recognises it **on the device**, and a matcher decides between the answers the activity expects: a clear match counts exactly like a tap, a half-match is checked with the child, and anything unclear is never wrong; it is asked again, then the cards invite a tap. Everything is optional: with voice off, nothing changes and no microphone code runs.

What M5 does **not** do: the AI tutor and its live voice (M6); the full parent controls and the alert word lists (M7; M5 adds one voice switch, see Q7); voice for "give N" and drag templates; open conversation, explain-back and the off-topic redirect (M6 and later); a child-adapted model; iPhone and iPad (the iOS gate comes later, the engine is the same code).

## 2. How we verify

Almost everything is tested without a microphone: the matcher is pure Dart and is run over thousands of Hebrew variants as text; the engine sits behind a small interface with a **fake engine** that returns scripted hypotheses (so each state of the mic, each repair step and each decision is a widget test); a fake microphone feeds the real engine from WAV files. The only things that need real speech are the **accuracy gate** (97%), which needs recordings (Q6), and the on-phone feel. The model itself is checked on the phone and, because `sherpa_onnx` also runs on Windows, on this PC over WAV files.

## 4. Tasks, in order

### Phase A — The matcher (`app/lib/speech/`, pure Dart)

- **A1.** `hebrew_text` pieces: strip niqqud, fold final letters, remove one-letter prefixes when the rest is a known word, digits to values, a consonant-skeleton **phonetic key** with the confusable sets of tech.md (כ/ח, ת/ט, ק/כ, א/ע/ה, ב/ו, ר→ל) and child substitutions (שָׁלוֹס for שָׁלוֹשׁ).
- **A2.** Accept-lists: numbers 0 to 20 (both genders, construct forms, digit, number plus noun), colours (four forms; near-misses like תְּכֵלֶת for כָּחֹל are **soft**), yes and no, and the global intents (again, help, stop). Built from the option's value or its `speech` words.
- **A3.** `AnswerMatcher.decide(hypotheses, confidences, expected, band)` → `accept | confirm | retry | noSpeech` with the matched option, scores `confidence^0.5 × similarity`, a required margin over the next-best answer, thresholds accept 0.75 and confirm 0.50 (tunable per item), the wrong-answer rule of Q9.
- **A4.** `test/answer_matcher_test.dart`: table-driven over thousands of generated variants (every form of every accepted word, with prefixes, final letters, digits, plausible child errors), plus property tests: silence and noise never produce `accept`; no input produces a wrong-answer `accept`; the best of two near answers must win by the margin.

### Phase B — Engine contract and the mic states

- **B1.** `SpeechEngine` (`load`, `listen(expected, band)` returning a stream of partial results and a final `Hypotheses{texts, confidences}`, `cancel`), `FakeEngine` for tests, and `ListenSession` (tap → listening → thinking → decision; silence limits per band; 8 s cap; the voice director is stopped first; partial result accepted after 600 ms of silence when stable; audio only ever in memory).
- **B2.** The **mic states of board 28** on the existing `MicButton`: ready, listening (ears up, live level ring), thinking (three dots; a filler line at 1.5 s; the rule-based path at 3.0 s), understood (the heard word echoed and the matching card lit), not understood. Character states `listen`, `think`, `encourage` from M4. Reduced motion supported. The button is hidden when voice is off.
- **B3.** The **repair sequence**, never more than two asks and never "wrong": (1) "my ears got mixed up, say it again"; (2) "I heard ׳שֶׁבַע׳, right?" with a replay, a tick and a thumbs-down, or the child says "כֵּן"; (3) the answer cards pulse and the mic fades, quietly. New core lines for these are added to `content/lines/core.he.json` (all `needs-editor`) and voiced through the M4 pipeline.
- **B4.** A per-child **voice reliability** score per answer type from a new table `voice_listen` (database version 5: when, answer type, outcome as a fixed word; no audio, no text, no transcript). When recognition keeps failing, the app stops inviting voice for that child and that answer type, quietly.

### Phase C — Wire it into the activities

- **C1.** `count_tap` and `tap_answer` get the mic: the question finishes, the mic is offered; a decided answer calls `chooseOption`; global intents map to replay, the lamp ("I don't know" gets praise and the help ladder, as the plan says) and home.
- **C2.** `attempt.input` (`tap` or `voice`) written with every answer; the engine of M3 is not changed.
- **C3.** Activity schema: option `speech` (optional list of accepted words, with niqqud); the validator lints it; the starter pack gets spoken words only where the number or label is not enough.

### Phase D — The microphone and the model (native)

- **D1.** `record` as the microphone, 16 kHz mono PCM into the engine in small chunks, echo cancellation and noise suppression on; the permission asked only from the parent's switch; the stream is closed the moment listening ends, and the app going to the background stops it.
- **D2.** `SherpaEngine`: loads the model files from the private folder after checking their SHA-256 against the hashes in the app; streaming decode in chunks; the result's tokens and confidences; the model is loaded when the parent turns voice on and when the app starts with voice on, off the main thread, so a lesson never waits for it.
- **D3.** `tools/make-m5-model.ps1`: downloads the four files from Hugging Face on your PC, prints their hashes, and with `-Push` copies them to the phone (same adb pattern as M4). The hashes go into the app config in the same commit.
- **D4.** Parent area: the **"Voice answers"** switch (Q7), the model status ("model installed" or "not installed"), the credits line (Q10).

### Phase E — The evaluation (needs you, see Q6)

- **E1.** `tool/asr_eval.dart` (PC): runs the real model and the real matcher over a folder of WAV files with a labels file (`file, intended answer, options`) and prints, per child and per band, the share of accepted answers that were right, the confirm and retry rates, the end-of-speech-to-decision time, and the worst misses. Works on Windows through `sherpa_onnx`'s desktop library.
- **E2.** (Dropped by the owner's answer to Q6: no recorder in the app. The recording instructions for the adults are in `docs/owner-todo.md`.)
- **E3.** Thresholds tuned on the recordings; the numbers go in the report with their limits.

### Phase F — Proof and report

- **F1.** All of the above green; goldens for the mic states in phone and tablet sizes; PLAN.md, PROGRESS.md and `docs/owner-todo.md` updated; on-phone steps in `docs/setup/android-device-checks.md` (install the model, turn voice on, answer by voice, try the repair steps).
- **F2.** Report against the acceptance criteria in the standard form, saying plainly which part of the 97% gate was and was not measured.

## 9. Risks and unknowns

- **Young children's Hebrew.** The model was trained on adult speech; for ages 3 and 4 it may fail the gate. The design already lets that be harmless: closed answer sets, the confirm step, tap always available, and quiet withdrawal of the voice invitation per child. The report will say what the data shows per band, and "voice for Band A" stays a decision from the data (PLAN open decision 4).
- **sherpa hotwords with this model** are unverified (tech.md open decision 3). The matcher does not depend on them; I will test whether they help and keep them only if they do.
- **Native build risk.** The first build with two native packages can fail in CI (ABIs, Gradle, the minimum Android version). It is checked in the first commit of Phase D, before anything is built on it.
- **APK size and memory.** The model is 72 MB on disk and more in memory; on a low-end phone loading may be slow or fail. The model loads only with voice on, off the main thread, and a failure leaves voice unavailable, never the lesson broken. I have no low-end phone to prove it.
- **Noise and echo.** The tutor's own voice must not be heard as the child (the line stops before listening). Real rooms are noisy; the gate recordings should be made in a normal room, not a quiet studio.
- **Permission flow.** Android's permission dialog is the OS's, and a "don't ask again" answer needs the parent to change it in system settings; the switch will say so in words.
- **Hebrew number forms.** The matcher is only as good as its accept-lists; colloquial forms ("שלוש כלבים") are accepted by design (PLAN), and the lists grow with the content.

**What was built**

- `app/lib/speech/`: Hebrew text tools (niqqud, final letters, prefixes, phonetic key), the lexicon (numbers 0 to 20 in both genders and the construct forms, ten colours with near misses, commands), the matcher (`accept`, `confirm`, `retry`, `noSpeech`), the engine contract, `VoiceAnswerController` (mic states and the repair sequence), the reliability rule, `SherpaEngine` and the model's pinned files.
- Database version 5: `attempt.input` (`tap` or `voice`) and `voice_listen` (when, kind of answer, outcome as a fixed word; no audio and no text), with a migration test from version 4. A child for whom voice keeps failing is quietly not offered the mic (and is offered it again after a week).
- Screens: the mic button in the five states of board 28 (ready, listening with a ring and bars, thinking, understood, not understood, and faded), the tick and "again" of "did you say ...?", the half-heard card glowing, the cards lighting one after the other when voice did not work, the guide's listen, think and surprised poses; commands: "again", "I don't know" (welcomed like the eye) and "stop".
- Activities: `count_tap` and `tap_answer` listen; the option `speech` list (schema, validator, JSON Schema files) lets authors add words; for `tap_answer` the words of an option's label that its neighbours do not share are used ("אדום" for the red flower).
- Parent area: the "Voice answers" switch, a note when the model or the permission is missing, and the credit line of the model (CC-BY-4.0).
- Core lines: three new neutral lines with niqqud (`core.listen.mixed`, `core.listen.confirm`, `core.listen.tap`), in `content/lines/core.he.json` and `He`, so `voice_gen` voices them for every character.
- Tools: `tools\make-m5-model.ps1` (fetch the model at a pinned revision, check its hashes, push it to the phone), `tools\run-asr-eval.ps1` and `app/tool/asr_eval.dart` (measure model and matcher on WAV recordings). The Windows run uses the x64 libraries of the same sherpa-onnx release (v2.0.0), checked against a pinned SHA-256; the pub.dev package ships only the ARM ones.

**Differences from the plan**

- The recogniser gives no confidence value, so every reading is given the same confidence (0.85) and the decision rests on how well the words match. Per-token confidence may exist in a later sherpa-onnx; the matcher already takes one.
- The streaming model decodes in chunks of over a second, so after 500 ms of silence the engine pushes 1.5 s of silence into the stream to read the last word at once (the partial result that the early-accept rule needs).
- The model is loaded on the main isolate (the first load blocks for about a second or two, to be measured on the phone); it happens when voice is first used, not during a lesson's first frame. Moving it to a background isolate is a known follow-up if it shows.
- Weight of the native code: the arm64 libraries are 27 MB uncompressed in the package (tech.md budgets 15 MB for all native libraries); the debug APK built here is 250 MB because it carries every ABI unstripped. A release build for arm64 only is the way to keep it small (M9).

**Still open**

- On-phone checks (device checks section 9): put the model on the phone, turn voice on, answer by voice, try the repair steps, and time the first load.
- Moving the model load off the main isolate if it janks; a real confidence if sherpa-onnx offers one.

The mic is always offered while the parent's switch is on; each question still falls back to tapping after two unclear tries.
