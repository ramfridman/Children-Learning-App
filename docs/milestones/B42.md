## 1. In short

- Once a day, the first time a child's map appears, the friend the child chose greets the child **by name**, says in one
  short sentence **what we will do today** (the topic of the stop Today's Adventure picked), and points at the bottle.
  About 5 to 8 seconds. Any tap stops it. Nothing about it ever counts days, missed days or streaks.
- **The set is finite and name-free: 48 fixed lines, 1,234 characters a voice** (unpointed voice text; at most 2,013 if
  every mark were sent). 4 greetings, 37 topics (every one of the 100 skills is mapped exactly once: 92 to a topic, 8 to
  their land line; checked by script; the table is `content/lines/welcome-topics.json`), 6 land lines (fallback), 1
  closing line. No line says the child's name or anything about the child.
- **The name is a separate short clip**, made once per child per speaking voice through the gateway from a spelling the
  parent checked, cached on the device only, and played right before the greeting. This is exactly the owner's Q6 pattern
  for `homeBubble`, so **the same name clip serves both** and no fixed line is ever voiced per child. With no name clip, the
  same greeting plays alone and is still a whole sentence: nothing is voiced twice, nothing waits for the name.
- **The 48 lines join the one paid run (B-45)**: about 0.44 USD for all nine voices, 0.10 USD if only the two narrators say
  them. Recommended: the friend's own voice (decision 2).
- **Found on the way (important for "one run"):** the 25 bottle lines of B-39 (`vo:core.adventure.*`) and the shared clip of
  `homeBubble` ("מה בבקבוק?") are owner-approved or text-ready, but `voice_gen` does not read them (it reads only the pack
  and `content/lines/core.he.json`). As B-45 stands they would stay silent after the one run. They are 26 clips, 909
  characters a voice. Recommended: put them into the same run with the welcome (decision 3).

## 2. What the child hears and sees, and when

### 2.1 When

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Once a day per child | The first time that child's **home map** is shown and settled on a day. The day is `Engine.dayKey` (the same UTC day as the engine and B-39; in Israel it turns at 02:00 or 03:00, inside the rest hours). The day is written **when the welcome starts**, so leaving, a crash or a skip never brings it back the same day. |
| Per device | Stored with the profile on the device (a day number, nothing else). A child who plays on two devices can be welcomed once on each; it is not synced (no new sync field for a greeting). |
| Not on top of something else | Not while the friend choice, the placement warm-up, a ceremony, the rest scene (the parent's time is up) or the parent area is showing. If the first map of the day comes later, the welcome comes then. Never during a lesson, never at the end of one. |
| Not when there is nothing to say | Today's Adventure reason `nothing` (no lesson in any open land): the name and the greeting only, no topic, no closing line. |
| Not as a reaction to absence | The welcome is the same after one day or after a month. It never depends on the gap since the last visit. |
| Coming back the same day | Coming back from a lesson, switching child and back, reopening the app: the ordinary map, no welcome. |

### 2.2 What plays (the order)

```
[name clip, if one exists]  greeting  ·  topic or land line  ·  "בּוֹאוּ נִפְתַּח אֶת הַבַּקְבּוּק."
```

Example (rotation 1, a Band A child whose bottle holds the next Counting Hills lesson, NUM-A-05):
"<name>, שָׁלוֹם! אֵיזֶה כֵּיף לְהִתְרָאוֹת. הַיּוֹם נִרְאֶה כַּמָּה יֵשׁ בְּמַבָּט אֶחָד. בּוֹאוּ נִפְתַּח אֶת הַבַּקְבּוּק."

**The composer (a pure function, like `todaysAdventure`; the app-developer builds it, the qa-engineer tests it):**

```
welcomeLines(stop, today, hasNameClip, warmUpDue(land), topicOf(skill)):
  greeting = stop.reason == welcome ? hello.first : hello.(today mod 3 + 1)
  if stop.kind == none:  return [name?, greeting]
  skill = stop.kind == revisit ? stop.skillId
        : the first skill of stop.lessonId whose domain is the stop's land   (the B-39 land rule)
  middle = (stop.kind == lesson and warmUpDue(stop.land)) ? land.<land>      (the warm-up decides where to start)
         : topicOf(skill) ?? land.<land>                                       (a skill with no topic: the land line)
  return [name?, greeting, middle, close]
```

- `stop` is the same `todaysAdventure` result the bottle uses, computed when the map is built. If the bottle later picks
  another stop (the child played something else first), nothing is said about it: the welcome was an invitation.
- `topicOf` is data: the topic table of section 4 (which skill belongs to which topic). It lives next to the lines, not in
  code, so a new skill is one data row (section 7.3).
- No randomness: the greeting rotates by the day number, so tests are deterministic (the B-39 rule).

### 2.3 What is shown

- The friend's speech bubble on the map (the one that says `homeBubble` today) shows **one sentence at a time, the one being
  said**: first "<name>, <greeting>" (the name in its isolate, shortened by the sign's rule, as `homeBubble` does), then the
  topic, then the closing line. On a phone the map bubble is one line; the longest welcome sentence is 45 characters
  (`topic.shapes.position`), so the design-director checks the fit (decision 7, a hand-off, not a change of rule).
- During the closing line the Today's Adventure pill (it shows the bottle) glows once (no glow with reduce motion).
- After the last line the bubble goes back to `homeBubble`, as on any other entry.
- The topic sentence shows no picture of the answer and no score. A small picture of the land's sign next to the topic
  sentence is allowed (design-director's call); nothing in it may look like a test.

### 2.4 Skip, stop, fallbacks

| Case | What happens |
|---|---|
| Any tap anywhere | The voice stops at once (a short fade), the bubble goes to `homeBubble`, and the tap does what it always does (a tap on a land opens the land). No skip button, nothing to learn. |
| Leaving the map | Stops; it counts as given for the day. |
| No name clip (not made yet, the parent never checked the spelling, the friend changed, the gateway is off) | The same sequence without the name clip. The caption still shows the name, as `homeBubble` does. |
| A fixed clip missing (no voice pack yet, the pack is older than the lines) | Text only for the whole welcome (no half-voiced sequence): each sentence shows for about 2.2 s (the B-39 `BottleHold` time), then `homeBubble`. |
| Sound off in the app's settings, or the parent turned voice off | Text only, as above. |
| No usable name (`displayChildName` gives none) | No name clip and a caption without a name, exactly like `homeBubbleNoName`. |

### 2.5 By band (the lines are the same; the pace and the support differ)

| | Band A (3 to 4) | Band B (5 to 6) | Band C (7 to 8) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What carries the message | the voice; the bubble is for the parent | the voice and the short pointed caption | the caption can carry it alone |
| Gap between clips | about 500 ms | about 350 ms | about 300 ms |
| The pointer | the pill glows and the friend turns towards it (design) | the pill glows | the pill glows |
| The topic | from the stop, so from the child's own level: a Band A child hears Band A topics | Band A or B topics, by mastery per land | no Band C content exists; the topic of whatever A or B skill the path offers; future Band C skills map to a topic or get the land line |
| Length | about 5 to 6 s with the name | the same | the same |

Placement is per land, by mastery (PLAN.md), so the band of a profile chooses only the pace. The topic always follows
the stop, never the age.

### 2.6 What the welcome never does (CLAUDE.md › Child safety, B-39 section 8)

No count of days or visits, no streak, no "come back", no "we missed you", no "you did not play", nothing that fades,
breaks or is lost, no timer, no comparison with a sibling, no question about the child ("מה נשמע?" is a personal question
and is not used), no praise of the child's traits. "הַיּוֹם" appears only as the plan of the visit ("today we will"), never
as "only today", the same use as the approved `core.goodbye.next`. Every line is "we" or "let's", so no line guesses a
gender, and the friend's one first-person line (`hello.3`, "יֵשׁ לִי") has no gender either.

## 3. The name

### 3.1 The slot rule

- The name is **always first** and **always its own clip**: `<name>,` directed as a call (a short rise and a small pause),
  then a full, name-free sentence that starts with "שָׁלוֹם". This is the owner's Q6 decision for `homeBubble`
  (hebrew-notes D15; the voice director's note: "a name alone, never inside any other line").
- Only the four greeting lines may follow a name clip. A topic, land or closing line never does. So the name is
  heard once per welcome.
- One name clip per child **per speaking voice** (decision 2): the friend the child chose. A new friend needs a new clip;
  until it exists the welcome plays without the name.
- The same clip is played before the shared `homeBubble` clip "מה בבקבוק?", so a child needs **one** name clip in all.

### 3.2 The path (the security-engineer reviews it; the app-developer builds it)

The device never sends the name. Security rule 2 says devices send ids, numbers and enums only, so the server must hold
what is voiced:

1. **The spelling.** The parent writes how the name is said (with nikud if needed) in the parent area, next to the
   nickname. Stored on the server with the child (`app.child_profile`, a new column, for example `name_spoken`, 1 to 24
   characters, Hebrew letters, nikud, space, hyphen and geresh only, checked with the same rules as `safeChildName` of
   B-32). Synced with `child-put` like the nickname. Deleted with the child.
2. **The request.** A new gateway function (for example `name-voice`): the four checks of security rule 3, parent role,
   body `{child_id, character_id}` only. The server reads the spelling and the character's voice id, sends `"<spelling>,"`
   to ElevenLabs with the pack's model, format (`mp3_44100_64`) and settings, and returns the audio. It stores no audio,
   logs no name (the family, the character count and the cost only), deletes the ElevenLabs history item (the nightly job
   of M6), and is under a cap per family per day and a kill switch.
3. **The cache.** The device keeps the clip in its encrypted store, keyed by child, character and a hash of the spelling;
   a changed spelling or friend makes the old clip unused and deletes it. Never synced, never in a backup to the server,
   never in the repo, a pack or a log (SEC-5).
4. **When it is made.** Only when a parent taps "listen to the name" in the parent area (he hears it first and can fix
   the spelling), decision 5. Never by Claude, never in a test (mocks only), never in the B-45 run. Cost: about 5
   characters, 0.0002 USD a clip.

The model (Anthropic) never sees the name: the welcome is built on the device from ids and fixed clips; nothing goes to
the tutor.

### 4.1 Why topics, and why these (the curriculum reasoning)

- **A topic, not a land and not a lesson.** "We'll go to Counting Hills" (the land) says where, not what, and the bottle
  already says where (B-39). One line per lesson (183 lessons) or per skill (100; the skills' `childLine` texts would be a
  ready source) is not small. A topic is a family of skills that one honest sentence describes: 37 for 100 skills, 3 to 8
  per land.
- **Every topic sentence must be true for every skill under it.** Where one sentence would have overpromised (zero is not
  "big numbers", a growing pattern is not "a pattern that repeats", "equals" is not "sharing"), the skill got its own
  topic or a wider sentence. Checked by script: 100 of 100 skills mapped, each exactly once, in its own land (92 to a
  topic, 8 to the land line after the re-check of section 7.2).
- **The sentences name an action the child will do** ("we will count", "we will sort", "we will tell the robot"), in the
  words of the skills' own `childLine` where they exist, so the welcome, the map card and the lesson use one vocabulary.
- **Teaching care in a few lines:**
  - `market.equal` says the meaning the skill teaches (equals means the same on both sides, the cure for "= means write the
    answer", curriculum §5.2), and avoids "מה זה שווה", which in the market land reads as "what is it worth".
  - `robots.fix`: the mistake is the robot program's, never the child's (debugging framed as fixing a plan).
  - `shapes.position` has no left or right (Band A).
  - No topic line contains a number the lesson might not reach ("to ten", "to twenty"), so a line stays true when a path
    changes.
- **The land line is the safety net.** A Band B or C child whose warm-up for that land is due may be placed past the
  path's next lesson, so the topic would guess; the land line is always true. A future skill with no topic row also gets
  its land line, so a new pack never needs a voice run before it can ship (section 7.3).

### 7.2 Where the lines live

One source that `voice_gen` reads and the master table lists: a lines file such as `content/lines/welcome.he.json` (the
`core.he.json` format: `id`, `text`), plus the topic table (`topic` -> skill ids). The bottle lines expanded per land can go
in the same or a sibling file. The app's captions must say the same words (either the app reads the file, or `he.dart`
holds them with a test that the two agree); the app-developer chooses.

**The topic table: `content/lines/welcome-topics.json` (format `welcome-topics/v1`, curriculum-expert, 2026-11-03).**
The editor's 48 lines are in `core.he.json` (`welcome.*`, vo id = `vo:` + id). The table beside them is data only (no
text a child sees or hears):

```
{ "schema": "welcome-topics/v1", "note": ..., "checked": {"by", "date", "pack", "skills"},
  "lands": [ { "land": "counting", "domain": "numbers", "line": "vo:welcome.land.counting",
               "topics": [ { "line": "vo:welcome.topic.counting.count", "skills": ["NUM-A-01", ...] }, ... ],
               "landLine": [] }, ... ],                      // the six lands in map order (TrophyLand)
  "why": { "<skill id>": "<reason>" } }                     // every skill sent to the land line or moved
```

Rules (what the app-developer's test of work item 4a checks): `land` is a `TrophyLand` name and `domain` its skill domain
(`app/lib/rewards/award.dart`); every `line` is a `welcome.*` id of `core.he.json`, a topic's under
`vo:welcome.topic.<land>.`; every skill of the installed pack appears **exactly once**, in the land whose domain is its
own, either in one topic's `skills` or in `landLine`; no topic is empty (no voiced line is unused). `topicOf(skill)` is the
topic line whose `skills` holds the skill, else null, so the composer says the land line (section 2.2) for a skill in
`landLine` and for a skill that is not in the file at all (a future pack; the test lists it so that it gets mapped). No
schema file in `content/schemas`: that folder is generated from the pack's Dart types (`pack_builder schema`), and the
lines files have none either; the app-developer may add one when the loader is written.

## 11. Evidence and sources

- **The child's own name catches attention from infancy:** 4.5-month-olds prefer their own name to stress-matched names
  (Mandel, Jusczyk and Pisoni 1995, *Psychological Science* 6(5); pages given as 314-317 or 315-318 in different indexes).
  This supports using the name as the call that opens the welcome, said once.
- **Telling learners what comes (advance organisers) has mixed evidence:** the classic meta-analysis found small effects
  (Luiten, Ames and Ackerson 1980, *American Educational Research Journal* 17, 211-218; later reviews call the field
  inconclusive), and almost none of it is with children of 3 to 8. So the topic sentence is a warm transition and a
  preview, not a claimed learning gain; it stays one short sentence.
- **Praise the process, not the person** (Gunderson et al. 2013, *Child Development* 84(5), 1526-1541): no welcome line
  praises the child's traits.
- **No contingent rewards, streaks or day counts** (Deci, Koestner and Ryan 1999, *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6),
  627-668; already the basis of B-39 section 9).
- **Choice supports motivation** (Patall, Cooper and Robinson 2008, *Psychological Bulletin* 134(2), 270-300): the welcome
  suggests, the map stays open, and a tap anywhere goes where the child wants.
