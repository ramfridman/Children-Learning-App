The course is for **any child at any level** (owner, 2026-11-03): a child who has never heard English, one who understands some, and one who already reads. Nothing here assumes one family.

## 1. How to read this file

- **Stages 1-7** are the content path every child walks, in order, from the first word to short texts (research §5.1):
  1 First words · 2 Talking and sound play · 3 Sounds and letters · 4 Reading words · 5 More sounds · 6 Reading sentences · 7 Reading and writing short texts.
- **Skill ids** `ENG-<band>-<nn>` carry the band where the skill is usually first taught (A 3-4, B 5-6, C 7-8). A child is placed by mastery, not by age: a 7-year-old who never heard English starts at `ENG-A-01` with Band C presentation (frame §3.1, the presentation band is separate from the content level).
- **Sub-skills.** A skill taught group by group is split into real skill nodes with dotted ids (`ENG-B-19.G1`, `.G2`; `ENG-C-15.G3`, `.G4`). The plain id is a **roll-up**: it has no BKT of its own, is fresh or durable when all its groups are, and as a prerequisite means "all groups" (frame §3.3.2).
- **Letter bundles (D16).** A letter group's sound, name, shape and writing open together as one bundle and count as **one** new skill (frame §3.3.3): `B-16` with `B-19.G1`; `B-17` with `B-19.G2`; `C-01` with `C-15.G3`; `C-02` with `C-15.G4`. Bundle members never require each other.
- **Strands:** LIS listening and vocabulary · SPK speaking and chunks · PA phonological awareness and the new English sounds · PH phonics (letters, sounds, names, decoding) · RD reading for meaning · WR writing (letter formation, spelling, sentences).
- **Mastery** follows `docs/curriculum.md` §10.2 and frame §3.3 (fresh mastery: P(L) ≥ 0.95, at least 4 successes over at least 2 days, at least 2 representations, an understanding check; durable mastery after a delayed check at least 7 days later with a different item; **set coverage**: every word of a set answered right once without a hint). "Mastery is seen" says what the app counts. 2-choice items count at most 50% of the evidence.
- **Speaking skills** (SPK) get **per-word feedback** from an English on-device recogniser when a parent turns voice on for English (D1, frame §5.1): **confident** (real praise naming the word) or **unsure** ("let's say it together" and a model of the word), never "wrong". Confident gives capped evidence (at most 50% of a speaking skill's success evidence, representation `embodied`); unsure gives none. The rest comes from comprehension taps, bubble choices and the parent's weekly "did you hear …?" confirmation. With the microphone off, a speaking skill that has none of these stays **"practised"** for the parent, never "not mastered". Speech never counts for a reading skill and is never a placement probe.
- **Errors**: the tags in the last column are defined in §8 (typical errors of Hebrew speakers; kind and family as in frame §4.2).
- **Templates** use the shared ids of frame §2: **LS01** (text and sound options, on `tap_answer` "tap", `match_pairs` "pairs", `sort_drag` "sort", `build_pattern` "order", `spot_error` "spot", with the text-stem and cloze modes), **LS02** write a letter (trace → copy → memory), **LS03** sound boxes (drum mode in Band A), **LS04** blend slide, **LS05** change one part (optional for English), **LS06** build a word, **LS07** build a sentence, **LS08** story (`listen`, `dialogic`, `read_along`, `read_first`, `echo`), **LS09** say it with me, **LS10** puppet talk (with the personal mode), **LS11** listen and do, **LS12** sing along, **LS13** placement, **LS14** writing tray (later). Existing templates kept as they are: **T02** `count_tap` and **T18** `clock_set`. `flash` (T14) is never used for reading.

## 2. Scope and sequence by band

## 3. The phonics order (stages 3-6)

Systematic synthetic phonics adapted for Hebrew speakers (research §5.3). Sounds are General American (D5). Lowercase first. **Every letter is taught whole (D16):** its shape, its sound, its name and its writing are introduced together (frame §5.3), and practice mixes the four. A group is mastered when its letters are known both ways (sound and name), its words are read and built, and its letters are written from memory; one group at a time; earlier groups are revisited in every session's warm-up.

**Writing inside each group.** The letters of a group are written in LS02 in the order of their formation families, so the stroke cue is shared: round letters c a d g o q start at "2 o'clock" and go anticlockwise; s e f; l i t j (down strokes); r n m h b p k (down, then up and over); u y; v w x z (slants). Q is written with group 4 (qu).

### 3.2 Blending and segmenting (how a word is read and built)
1. **Oral first** (B-14, B-15): the robot says the sounds, the child finds the word; then the child splits a word into sounds with tokens. No letters.
2. **Sound buttons** (B-18 onward, LS04 `graphemes`): each letter of a word has a dot (a bar under a digraph); the child slides a finger **left to right**, each sound plays, then the whole word plays. Continuous sounds (s, m, n, f, l, v, z, sh, th) are stretched into the next sound ("sssaaat"), stops are clipped ([MoE-PF]: "read each phoneme aloud correctly, blending"). **Blending always uses sounds, never letter names** ("sat", not "ess-ay-tee"; frame §5.3).
3. **Build** (B-20 onward, LS06): hear the word, put the letters in left-to-right slots; the slots light one by one as the sounds are said.
4. **Read for meaning**: the word or sentence is matched to a picture; this is what the engine counts.
5. **Decodable only**: a word is used for reading only when all its graphemes are taught, or it is a taught tricky word (lesson-review §3; the F5 validator check).

### 3.3 Tricky words (common words with an unusual part)
Taught as whole words, with the tricky part pointed out ("the 'o' in 'to' says /u/").

### 3.4 Letter names and the alphabet
- **Names are taught with sounds for every letter (D16, frame §5.3).** The introduction of a letter is: see it big; hear its **sound** in a key word with a picture (stops clipped, no "uh"); hear its **name** as a separate fact ("its name is s, it says /s/"); watch it drawn and trace it (LS02); find it among 2 or 3 letters and pick a picture that starts with its sound. Name ↔ letter items are part of each group's mastery evidence.
- **The child is never asked to read by names**: blending uses sounds (§3.2).
- **Letters whose name does not carry their sound** get an explicit "name …, sound …" line and a NAME-family distractor (`M-ENG-LETTERNAME`) in their items: **h** /eɪtʃ/ → /h/, **w** /ˈdʌbəlju/ → /w/, **y** /waɪ/ → /j/, **c** /si/ → /k/, **g** /dʒi/ → /ɡ/, **x** /ɛks/ → /ks/ (frame §5.3), and in English also the five **vowels**, whose names are their long sounds while the first sound taught is the short one: **a** /eɪ/ → /æ/, **e** /i/ → /ɛ/, **i** /aɪ/ → /ɪ/, **o** /oʊ/ → /ɑ/, **u** /ju/ → /ʌ/ (adopted, frame §4.4 E4). **The limit:** a vowel's name is a `M-ENG-LETTERNAME` distractor only where the target is the **short** sound, B-16 to C-07. From the open-syllable and final-e skills (C-06, C-08) the long sound, the vowel's name, is right ("the e makes the vowel say its name"), so it is never a NAME distractor there; the errors there are `M-ENG-OPEN` (he read as /hɛ/) and `M-ENG-E-SILENT` (cape read as cap).
- **Names in General American:** a /eɪ/ · b /bi/ · c /si/ · d /di/ · e /i/ · f /ɛf/ · g /dʒi/ · h /eɪtʃ/ · i /aɪ/ · j /dʒeɪ/ · k /keɪ/ · l /ɛl/ · m /ɛm/ · n /ɛn/ · o /oʊ/ · p /pi/ · q /kju/ · r /ɑɹ/ · s /ɛs/ · t /ti/ · u /ju/ · v /vi/ · w /ˈdʌbəlju/ · x /ɛks/ · y /waɪ/ · z /zi/ ("zee", D5).
- **Digraphs are named by their letters**: "s-h says /ʃ/", "c-k says /k/".
- **The alphabet song and alphabet order** (B-22) come once names are known for group 1; the song covers all 26 names receptively. Capitals from stage 5 (C-13).

### 3.5 Spelling and accent
American spelling and General American sounds are decided (D5): color, gray, mom, favorite, pants, cookie, candy; rhotic r-controlled vowels; short o /ɑ/. British forms are not used anywhere in the course.

"What's your name?", "How old are you?", "What do you like?" and "Who is in your family?" are first **heard** much earlier, in the scripted personal turns of Band A (below), where the puppet models its own answer first.

**Personal questions (D10, frame §5.2).** Scripted lesson turns (LS10 personal mode only) may ask the child's **name, age, likes and who is in the family**. The puppet always answers first; the child answers by tap (pictures for likes, numbers 3 to 9 for age, family pictures with many shapes of family and "someone else") or by voice if voice is on; there is always a "pass". The answer is heard and used on the device for the next line only: it is never checked, scored, stored, synced, logged or used to choose content, and the puppet's reply is generic ("Nice to meet you!"), echoing only a target word ("Five!", "Apple!"), never the child's name. "Where do you live?", addresses, cities, the gan or school, phone numbers and photos are never asked. A parent switch turns personal turns off (Q-F2). The AI tutor never asks anything personal and is off in language lessons (frame §1.6).

### 4.3 How a word is taught (every word, every band)
1. Heard with its picture and, where possible, an action (never translated first).
2. Heard again in a chunk ("It's a cat."), then in a song or story.
3. Chosen among 2-3 pictures (listening), then among 3-4 with a sound or meaning neighbour (distractor with a tag).
4. Said (LS09; per-word feedback when voice is on); in later stages read and built.
5. Revisited by the spaced schedule (1 → 3 → 7 → 21 → 60 days, [Curr]); at least 6 meaningful encounters before a check of durable mastery; every word of a set answered right once without a hint before the set is fresh (set coverage, frame §3.3.4).

Hint ladder per item (curriculum §9.2, frame §2.3): **H1** the English again, slowly (the slow clip), with the word in a chunk where one exists; **H2** a new representation: the Hebrew word first, then the English again with the picture (D7: a Hebrew translation only at hint level 2, after the English was heard with a picture), or the action or the sound the thing makes; **H3** "show me": the friend picks it and says it, then the child does it.

## 5. The writing sequence

Handwriting goes **trace → copy → write from memory** (D4): tracing alone teaches little; writing from memory is what builds reading. LS02 has the three modes; a writing skill is mastered only with evidence in memory mode, and feedback comes at the end of a stroke, never during it.

| Step | Stage | What the child does | Template | Skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | 2 | Pre-writing: follows a path left to right (a car on a road, a dotted wave), top to bottom | LS02 trace mode with shapes, not letters | (warm-up for B-19) |

| W4 | 4 | Builds words with letter tiles (no handwriting load) | LS06 | B-20, C-16 |
| W5 | 5 | Capitals: trace, copy, memory; matches; capital for I, names, sentence starts | LS02, LS01 pairs, LS01 spot | C-13 |

### 6.2 The oral gate (before letters; frame §3.3.6)
A child starts the literacy strand when **(a)** the stage 1-2 listening core has fresh mastery (A-01 to A-11, A-16, B-01, B-02, B-05, B-06: about 100 words and the stage 1-2 chunks understood) and **(b)** B-12, B-13 and B-14 onset-rime have fresh mastery. With Band B presentation the gate needs durable mastery of (a); with Band C presentation fresh mastery is enough, so a grade-3 beginner reaches letters in about 20-30 sessions, close to the Ministry's 3-4 months ([MoE-PF]). The gate never opens under Band A presentation. A parent's "oral only" switch keeps it closed (D6). A child placed above the gate has it marked open. The gate holds skills back; the child never sees it as a lock.

### 6.3 The placement game (LS13)
The shared staircase of frame §3.2, as a "visit to the English island", with **two strands**:
- **O, oral**: listening (pictures), then chunks. Levels follow E0 → E1: A-01/A-03 words → A-05/A-09 words → A-11/A-16 chunks → B-01/B-02/B-05 words → B-06 positions → B-08/B-10 chunks and phrases. The probes are ordinary LS01 items with `purpose: probe` (the Band A probes are marked in `band-a-content.md` §5).
- **L, literacy**: offered only to a child with Band B or C presentation whose parent ticked "knows some English letters" in the short questionnaire. Levels follow E2 → E4: letter sounds of groups 1-2 → CVC reading (B-18) → group 3-4 letters → digraph words (C-04) → a decodable sentence (C-17).
- **Rules:** at most **8 items per strand**; two right answers go up a level, one wrong goes down; stop after 2 reversals or 8 items; the placement is the highest level with at least 2/3 right, minus one unit; a strand whose first two probes fail at its floor stops at once; the strands run across the first 2-3 visits, oral first, never more than about 8 probe items in one visit; **speech is never a probe** (speaking is set by the parent's answer); the questionnaire sets where a staircase starts, never where it ends.
- Skipped skills are "assumed" (prior 0.85) and checked by interleaved retrieval within 2 weeks; a failed check becomes a normal target with no penalty shown. Every probe gets warm feedback; no result is shown to the child. The parent sees "we start here" per strand and can move it one level. The oral gate (§6.2) is checked after placement.

## 7. Sessions and Hebrew support

### 7.1 Session shape (frame §3.5, the English defaults)
| Band | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| A | 6-8 min | hello chant (LS12) → 2 retrieval items → 1 new word set (3-4 words, LS01/LS11) → say it (LS09) → chant or story (LS12/LS08) → goodbye |
| B | 10-12 min | hello routine → 3 retrieval items → one new item of one strand (words or sounds or a letter bundle) → practice in 2 representations → a chunk role-play (LS10) or a chant → goodbye |
| C | 12-15 min | routine chunk → 4 retrieval items (mixed strands) → new phonics or reading skill → build or write → read (sentence or story) → goodbye |
At most 2 new skills per session (a letter bundle counts as one); never a new letter group and a new word set together in Band B. Revisits stay inside the English course; confusable pairs (b/d, v/w) are revisited together.

### 7.2 Hebrew around the English ("Hebrew frame, English target", D7)
- The **instruction** of an item is Hebrew at first ("Hebrew frame", hebrew-text-editor), the **target** is English, voiced as separate clips (`voice-brief.md` §3; frame F3).
- **Ten English classroom phrases** replace the Hebrew instruction step by step (with the same icon, and the Hebrew still on the replay button): Listen! (stage 1) · Look! · Touch the ___. · Find the ___. · What's this? (stage 2) · Read. · Build the word. · Write. (stage 3-4) · Read the sentence. · Put the words in order. (stage 6).
- **A Hebrew translation** appears only at hint level 2, after the child heard the English with a picture (D7).
- **Explicit contrasts** in Hebrew, once each, when a skill starts: English goes left to right (B-18); English has big and small letters (C-13); this sound is not in Hebrew (B-11, C-02, C-03), with a mouth picture.
- **Praise and "not yet"** stay in Hebrew, specific and naming the English word ("נכון! זה cat"); a few English praise chunks ("Well done!", "Great!") are taught as words.
- **Parent co-play cards** (D9) in the reading stages (B-16 on): a short card after a letter bundle or a story, with something to do together off screen ([McT20]: phonics apps help most with an adult involved).
