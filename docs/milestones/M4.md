## 1. Goal and "done when"

From PLAN.md, unchanged:

- Role-slot resolver; lite packs (Nitzi plus the 8 family characters); procedural animation; `voice_gen` for each character's core lines; playback with mouth movement.
- **Done when:** changing the companion changes the character and voice on every screen; 60 fps on the low-end Android.

M4 gives the app a face and a voice. Every screen asks for a **role** (guide, map companion, celebration cameo, sticker), never a character; a resolver fills the role with the child's chosen companion; the character is drawn from image layers and moves procedurally; and it speaks the lesson's lines in its own voice, with its mouth following the sound. The child's companion is stored on the child's profile and chosen on a Friends' Beach screen.

What M4 does **not** do: the AI tutor and live voice (M6), speech input (M5), the island hosts and the Hero Squad as playable companions (their art exists, but M4 builds Nitzi and the 8 family characters, as PLAN.md says; the pipeline makes adding the rest content-only), and a parent-area "Import character" screen (M7; see Q4).

## 2. How we verify

Tests drive everything with fakes: a fake audio player (so a test knows exactly which file played and when), a fake ElevenLabs server for `voice_gen`, and deterministic mock voices (a distinct tone per character) so that "the voice changed" is a testable fact. Goldens show two different companions on every screen. The real ElevenLabs run is a separate, owner-gated step (Phase H) because it spends money and needs your key and your ears.

## 4. Tasks, in order

### Phase A — Contract and resolver (`app/lib/characters/`)

- **A1.** `CharacterManifest` (`character-lite/v1`, as in tech.md 6.5 and your `pack.json` files): id, Hebrew name (with the `{name}` template for family characters), grammatical gender, roles, images per state, mouth anchor, palette, `personalUseOnly`, voice (provider voice id and settings, used by the tool only).
- **A2.** Roles: `guide_tutor`, `map_companion`, `celebration_cameo`, `activity_helper`, `sticker`. `CharacterResolver` fills a role: the child's companion if its manifest allows the role, else the domain friend, else the activity's default, else **Nitzi, always bundled**. Only available characters take part (installed, enabled, allowed in this build).
- **A3.** Progress never belongs to a character: stickers and the companion choice are keyed to the child and to roles; a removed pack falls back to Nitzi and earned stickers show as a silhouette (tested).
- **Check:** resolver tests for every priority and every fallback; a removed or disabled pack never breaks a screen.

### Phase B — Character packs

- **B1.** A new document kind in the pack format: `character.<id>.json`, plus the images as pack files (PNG or SVG), validated by the same installer (signature first, then hashes, then the schema). New schema, JSON Schema file generated, 30 broken-file cases like M2.
- **B2.** Validator rules: every required image present; PNG header size 1024×1024 (SVG: viewBox); mouth anchor inside the canvas; the Hebrew name has niqqud; at most 8 MB; a `personalUseOnly` pack has `distribution: personal`.
- **B3.** `pack_builder build-character <folder>` (reads your existing `pack.json` and images) and a **build guard**: a test fails if any `personalUseOnly` pack is among the bundled assets or listed in `pubspec.yaml`.
- **B4.** Nitzi as the bundled house character: a signed pack of the design-bundle SVG states, installed on first start like the starter pack.
- **Check:** your eight family folders build into eight valid signed packs; a changed byte is refused (the M2 exhaustive test, again, on a character pack).

### Phase C — Drawing and moving a character

- **C1.** `CharacterView(role, state, mood, lipSync)`: draws the state's image layer, cross-fades between states, never re-decodes (images cached per size class), and uses only `Transform` and `Opacity`.
- **C2.** Procedural animation (as tech.md 6.5): a breathing bob of 2 to 3% and a gentle sway; squash and stretch on landing; a hop with a small spark burst on `celebrate`; a head tilt on `think`; a lean towards the screen on `listen`; an optional blink overlay; hop-in and pop entrances. Reduced motion (the system setting) turns all of it down to a still pose and a cross-fade.
- **C3.** `LipSyncDriver`: reads the cue file against the playback position and flaps `talk_open` and `talk_closed` (and, for Nitzi, the three mouth openings) with a minimum of 70 ms per frame so that it never flickers.
- **Check:** a state-by-state widget test; a lip-sync test with a scripted cue file and a fake clock (mouth open exactly when the cue says); a frame-cost benchmark (build time per frame while talking, hopping and breathing, on the phone and tablet sizes).

### Phase D — Voice playback

- **D1.** `VoicePlayer` (interface; `audioplayers` implementation; a fake for tests). `VoiceBank`: a character's **voice pack** (a signed pack of `vo.<line id>.mp3` and `cue.<line id>.json`, listing which content lines it covers) gives the file for a `vo:` id; a line with no recording is simply silent and its caption stays.
- **D2.** `VoiceDirector`: speaks the line shown in the guide's bubble (prompt, feedback, hint, celebration), one at a time, never overlapping, stops when the child leaves the screen or taps home, repeats on the replay touch, and drives the guide's `talk` state and mouth.
- **D3.** The lesson (M2) says its lines through the director; the caption always shows, voice or not.
- **Check:** fake-player tests for order, no overlap, stop on leave, replay, a missing recording, and "the voice follows the companion" (the file played comes from the active character's pack).

### Phase E — `voice_gen` (runs on your PC only)

- **E1.** `dart run tool/voice_gen.dart plan | audition | generate | mock`.
  - `plan`: lists the lines of a character (lesson lines of the installed packs plus the core lines file `content/lines/core.he.json`), the characters per voice and the total, and the cost at the price you pass in. Writes nothing and calls nothing.
  - `audition <voice ids>`: one short sample per candidate voice (a few hundred characters in all), for you to listen to.
  - `generate <character> --confirm-chars N`: refuses unless N equals the planned total; calls ElevenLabs Eleven v4 with timestamps; writes the audio and the cue file per line; then builds the character's voice pack with `pack_builder`. Reads the key from the `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` environment variable; never prints, stores or logs it.
  - `mock`: makes the same files with deterministic tones (a different pitch per character) and cues from the text, free, for tests and for trying the app before real voices exist.
- **E2.** Pronunciation: a list of names and tricky words in the core lines file, respelled with niqqud; a line that sounds wrong is respelled and regenerated.
- **Check:** against a fake ElevenLabs server: the refusal without the right count, the request body (voice, model, text with niqqud, no names), the cue conversion from timestamps, the key never appearing in any output or file (a scan), and idempotence (a line already generated is not generated twice).

### Phase F — Companion on every screen

- **F1.** Database version 4: `profile.companion_id` (nullable; null means Nitzi) with a migration test.
- **F2.** Friends' Beach: after the first lesson (and from the parent area), the child picks a companion from the available characters (big pictures, the character waves when touched, no reading). A parent can change it later (M7 adds the full control).
- **F3.** The guide in the activity frame, the map companion on the home screen, the celebration cameo and the sticker silhouette all come from the resolver. The static Nitzi art baked into the M1 scenes is removed and the character is placed at the same board coordinates.
- **Check:** a widget test that changes the companion and checks the picture, the playing voice file and the mouth on the home map, the lesson, the celebration and the picker, in phone and tablet sizes; goldens with two companions.

### Phase G — Proof and report

- **G1.** All of the above green; goldens; the frame-cost numbers; `PLAN.md` and `PROGRESS.md`; on-phone steps (install the characters, switch companion, hear the mock voices).
- **G2.** Report in the standard form.

### Phase H — Real voices (owner-gated, can come after the report)

- **H1.** You pick voices from the audition samples and give me the voice ids; I show the plan (characters and cost) and wait for your typed confirmation; I generate; you listen to every line; wrong ones are respelled and redone.
- **H2.** Exact numbered PowerShell steps for you: create a generation key at ElevenLabs with a spend limit, set it in the environment for one shell session, run `audition`, then `generate`.

## 9. Risks and unknowns

- **The art is a set of still images.** Nitzi's states are vector drawings; the family characters are single poses each. Procedural motion hides a lot but is not a rig; if it looks stiff on the phone, a Rive rig later is the upgrade, behind the same contract.
- **Mouth cues from timestamps.** Character timestamps are only as good as ElevenLabs' alignment for Hebrew. If a line's cue looks wrong on the phone, the fallback is a plain flap while the audio plays (still correct, less pretty). I will check a sample before building on it.
- **I cannot hear.** Voice choice, pronunciation, and "sounds wrong, respell" are yours; mock voices prove the machinery only.
- **No low-end phone.** Q9.
- **Names.** Family characters' names carry the child's name (`{name} הַמְּזַנֵּק`): shown in captions, but not voiced until M6, so their lines never say it.
- **Replacing Nitzi in the scenes.** The M1 board art has Nitzi baked into some SVGs; removing her and placing the live character at the same coordinates must not move anything (the M1 geometry tests guard it).
