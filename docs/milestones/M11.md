## 0. The plan in one page

1. **Three stages**, each merged and shown on the owner's phone on its own: the look (items 1, 3, 4); the lesson arc and content-only swaps (items 2, 5); new hands-on templates, one per step (item 7).

## 2. The lesson arc (a rule for the lands)

**Show → Together → Alone → Use.**

1. **Show** (unscored, the first screen of every lesson). The host does a worked example, narrated, and stops before the last step; the child does it. A wrong touch is "let's look again"; nothing is written. **Predict, then watch** for Logic, Robots and colour mixing: "what will happen?" (an unscored tap), then the host runs it. Full the first time a child meets the skill, then a short reminder the child can skip: worked examples help novices and get in the way of children who already know (expertise reversal).
2. **Together** (1–2 items, scored as today): the host does the first part, the child the rest.
3. **Alone** (3–5 items): at least two representations (§5.5 R-codes), hands-on before pictures before symbols (concreteness fading, Fyfe and others 2014).
4. **Use** (1 item): the idea in a new context (a story, the shop, the robot) or an understanding check (U-checks, §5.6).

**Band A:** Show, Together 1, Alone 2–3, Use optional; about 3 minutes. Bands B and C: the full arc, 4–6 minutes. Revisit missions and warm-ups keep their shapes.

**The second-hint rule (§5.5, amended).** Hint level 2 shows the idea in a **different representation** from the question and from level 1 (a ten-frame becomes fingers, a row becomes its bracketed unit). Lighting objects one by one is level 1 only. `hints[1]` names its R-code, never the activity's own `representation`.

### 2.1 Numbers: NUM-B-09, "sees quantities to 10 on a ten-frame (5 and 3 is 8)"
- **Show:** Nekudi fills a frame: a full row, "five"; three more below, "and three, eight". The child places the last counter (`give_n`, `frame`, unscored).
- **Together:** `give_n` frame, n = 7: Nekudi lays the first row of five; the child adds two.
- **Alone:** `flash` frames 6 and 9; `give_n` frame 8; a tap "how many?" on a 5-and-4 frame.
- **Use:** Nekudi's wings, 5 spots and 3 spots: how many? (R3).
- **Hint 2:** the frame becomes fingers, five on one hand (R4).

### 2.2 Shapes: SHP-A-03, "recognises shapes in any size and orientation"
- **Show:** Kubi runs a hand along a thin, upside-down triangle ("three straight sides, closed") and drops it in the triangle basket; the child drops a tilted one (`sort_drag`).
- **Together:** `sort_drag`, triangle and not-a-triangle; Kubi sorts the first.
- **Alone:** `sort_drag` with `shape_triangle_thin`, `shape_triangle_down`, `shape_open` (M-SHP-CLOSED), `shape_curved`, a big and a tiny triangle (M-SHP-SIZE); one tap "which is a triangle?" with only rotated ones (M-SHP-ORIENT).
- **Use:** find the triangle in a road sign (SHP-A-05 context).
- **Hint 2:** the outline is traced, its corners counted (R2 → R4).
- Why: non-prototypical examples and not-shapes are what change children's shape concepts (Clements and others 1999; Fisher and others 2013).

### 2.3 Logic: LOG-A-06, "extends an AB pattern"
- **Predict, then watch:** red, blue, red, blue, ?: "what comes next?" (tap, unscored); Timi lays the next two tiles; "red, blue: it repeats".
- **Together:** `build_pattern` extend: the row starts red, blue, red; Timi puts the next blue; the child continues.
- **Alone:** `build_pattern` extend with shapes, then with sizes; one tap item keeps M-PAT-NEXT.
- **Use:** fork, spoon, fork… on a table.
- **Hint 2:** the unit is bracketed and clapped: red-blue | red-blue (R2 → R4).

## 5. Keeping it cheap without lowering quality
- **Content only** where a template exists (2.4, 2.5, each Stage 3 batch).
- **One agent run per step and role**: CE writes a land's whole re-cut in one run, HE edits it in one.
- **One QA pass per stage**; the security-engineer reads only 2.1 and the host packs.
- **Goldens** of 9 rooms × 3 boards, with Show, celebration and the reaction's end state.
- **A headless playthrough per land, band and course** (scripted child, clips pending): unscored opening, arc order, caps, no empty hint.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

**Stage 1**
1. The 9 rooms' goldens differ pairwise in backdrop, frame colour and trail shape, also in greyscale.
2. In every land lesson and every course lesson the host appears, greets, and cheers in the celebration; the companion keeps the guide corner.
3. The stage reaction plays on every solved activity, also after a worked example, and never on a wrong answer; reduce motion shows the end state with no movement.
4. No new timer, counter of right answers, streak or loss; the reaction is not cumulative.

**Stage 2**
5. No land lesson's first item is scored; a Show writes no evidence (engine test).
6. Pick-only share (`tap_answer` with a choice, `match_pairs`, `spot_error`, `flash`; keypad answers do not count): **≤ 60% per skill in Bands A and B, ≤ 70% in Band C**; recognition skills where picking is the act (NUM-A-05, A-08, A-10, B-06, B-08, COL-A-01, SHP-A-02, LOG-A-02, LOG-B-06, B-09, MATH-B-16) **≤ 80%**; **≤ 50% per land and band**. Skills waiting for a Stage 3 template are listed by name with their step and exempt until it lands.
7. Every activity's hint level 2 has an R-code different from its own representation and from level 1, and draws a picture (none falls back to a generic one).
8. Every swapped activity has ≥2 tagged distractors or classifiers; the headless playthrough passes for every land and band.

**Stage 3 (per template)**
9. The template's own tests, goldens, reduce-motion form, worked example that stops for the child, and its skills meet criterion 6 without the exemption.

**Languages**
10. Each course's room distinguishable in a golden; its host greets and cheers; criterion 6 at 70% applies to language skills once L7 and the L5 batches land.
