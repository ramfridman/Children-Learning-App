## 1. Goal and "done when"

A parent can see how each child is really learning, set the rules, and be told when something needs attention. A second phone of the same parent receives alerts. Deleting a child removes them everywhere.

PLAN's acceptance for M7, in plain words:

1. **An alert reaches the parent's phone with no content in the push.** The push says only "יש התראה חדשה"; the details open behind the parent gate.
2. **Deleting a child leaves no rows for that child on the server,** and every device that knows the child deletes its local data.

Plus the criteria I will report against (each is proved by a test or a phone check, section 5):

3. **Controls work and are enforced on the child's side:** daily limit, weekly schedule, rest hours and bedtime lock with a gentle wind-down (never a hard cut in the middle of an activity); voice, AI helper (replacing the dev-page switch of M6) and address form; band and hidden domains; which characters each child may choose.
4. **The progress view tells the truth about the engine:** this week, understanding by domain, growth over time (the learning curve you asked for), what helped, misconceptions with a home activity, learning habits as strengths, and the tutor log of the last 30 days. Speed and raw error counts are never shown.
5. **The seven alerts are detected on the device** with no model, and only the alert (not the child's activity) syncs to the parent.
6. **Sync is correct:** a server sequence cursor, replay in the PLAN's order, settings last-write-wins, deleted children as tombstones, and rewards never taken away by a replay.
7. **Nothing leaks:** the child's words are kept only while "keep the words" is on (default on, 30 days, parent can turn it off); no child name or activity content in any push or log.

## 4. Tasks, in order

### Part 7a: on the device

**A. Settings and the rules the child side enforces**
- A1. Local settings per child and per family (Drift, new `parent_setting`): limits, schedule, rest hours, bedtime lock, voice, AI helper, address form, domain bands and hidden domains, allowed characters. The AI helper switch moves here from the dev page (the dev switch stays for tests).
- A2. The session clock (device time, with a small tolerance) and the gentle wind-down at the limit or in rest hours. Tests with a fake clock, including time zone and daylight changes.
- A3. Parent screens for all of it (Hebrew, RTL, behind the gate), with the gate and PIN of M1.

**B. The progress view**
- B1. Read-models over attempts and the engine replay: minutes per day, sessions, adventures completed, understanding by domain and skill (status in plain Hebrew from the skill documents), 8-week growth, what helped (representation statistics), misconceptions with status, learning habits as strengths.
- B2. Charts drawn by hand (`CustomPainter`, no chart package), each with its values written out (accessible).
- B3. The tutor log: the last 30 days of what the helper said, with a report button. In 7a it reads the device's own record of the lines it played; in 7b it can also read the server's `tutor_log`.

**C. Alerts on the device**
- C1. Detectors, pure and tested with scripted histories: past the limit or in rest hours; 3 failed gate attempts; guessing to farm rewards; frustration; inappropriate words; worrying disclosure; long absence or sudden drop. Each with severity (info, attention, important) and a one-line "what to do".
- C2. What the child hears for a worrying disclosure: a pre-recorded caring line that points to a trusted adult, then back to play (a new recording; I write the script, you approve the voice and cost first, as for M4).
- C3. The alert list screen. "Keep the words" switch, default on, 30 days.
- C4. The app never scolds and never says the child was "reported".

### Part 7b: sync, second phone, push, deletion

**D. Server**
- D1. Migration: `event` (server sequence, UUIDv7 id, family, child, device, client time, engine version, type, payload), `alert`, `push_token`, `consent`, `child_profile` (without a name unless Q9 says otherwise), `deleted_child` tombstones, with the RLS second layer and no grants to `anon` or `authenticated`.
- D2. Functions, each with the four checks of CLAUDE.md rule 3: `sync-push`, `sync-pull`, `alert-create` (child device), `alert-list` and `alert-read` (parent), `settings-get` and `settings-put`, `push-token-register` (parent), `child-delete` (parent, behind the gate), `tutor-log` (parent).
- D3. `alert-push`: sends the content-free push through FCM HTTP v1 with a service-account secret stored as a Supabase secret (never in the repo or app).
- D4. `pg_cron` retention: alerts and tutor logs 30 days, device rows 90 days after revoke, counters 90 days.
- D5. Backups: a scheduled GitHub Action runs `pg_dump` of the `app` schema, encrypts it with `age`, stores it privately, keeps 4.

**E. App**
- E1. The outbox on the child device: events and alerts leave in order, retry, never lose; cursor-based pull; replay in the PLAN's order (client time, device id, event id); replays rebuild the engine; rewards are their own events and are never taken away.
- E2. Parent device: `firebase_messaging` with auto-init off, started only after the device enrols as `parent`; registers its token; shows the alert list; the push opens the gate first.
- E3. Deleting a child: the parent confirms behind the gate; the server writes the tombstone and deletes the rows; every device deletes that child's local data on its next pull.

## 9. Risks and unknowns

- **False alerts and missed alerts.** Word lists in Hebrew are the weakest part: small, conservative, reviewed, and shown to the parent as "possible", never as fact.
- **Learning curve with little history.** The first weeks have few points; the view says "not enough yet" instead of drawing a flat line.
- **Pack version bump** (Q6) invalidates reviewed tutor lines unless the carry step exists; it is in the plan, with a test.
- **Clock tricks.** A child can change the phone's clock to beat the limit; the session clock uses a monotonic timer alongside the wall clock, and a large jump raises an info alert rather than a lock.
- **Firebase is a vendor with a Google SDK on a child-adjacent app.** It is started only on parent devices, receives only a token, and the push has no content; the app stays free of it on child devices (a test checks the child build never initialises it).
- **Sync is the hardest correctness piece** (late events, replays, deletion races); it is built after the controls so the parent area is useful even if it takes longer.
