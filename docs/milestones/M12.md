# M12: an app a small child is eager to play

Status: **Pilot approved by the owner on 2026-11-20** (Colour Bay first; he approves the pilot before the rest of the app; his answers are in section 8). Nothing is built yet.

## 1. Why

On 2026-11-19 the owner played Colour Bay with his 2-year-old daughter. His words: "this app is not a child friendly at all.. The
sounds are very robotic.. no celebrations, nothing that makes the child excited when he plays the app. We must improve the experience
completely to make this app something that children would eager to play and keep learning." And: "the sound only says a very not
exciting phrase on which color is this"; "some of the things she did fine and others wasn't very understandable to her and I had to
help her. It's probably because of how the lesson is taught and phrased and sounded."

Three reviews (2026-11-20) found the same thing from three sides. The details are in:

- `docs/milestones/M12-sound-notes.md` (voice-and-sound-director)
- `docs/design/m12/delight.md` and its four storyboards (design-director)
- `docs/milestones/M12-teaching-notes.md` (curriculum-expert)

What they found, in short:

1. **The app is silent except for the voice.** No touch sound, no chime, no music, no vibration anywhere. The sound palette of the
   design spec was never built; M11 put sound in "later".
2. **The voice is flat.** Every line is a short neutral read with no direction per kind of line. Nine characters share four voices.
   Goni, Colour Bay's host, has no voice at all, so his greeting was most likely a silent text bubble.
3. **Nothing reacts.** A tile has no pressed state. A right answer gives a pale tile, a small tick and a tiny hop. The only celebration
   is a text card at the end of a lesson.
4. **Too much before the first touch.** Four taps through three screens, 8 to 12 seconds of watching, things that need reading.
5. **The lessons test before they teach, in adult words.** Her first Colour Bay item asked for "the same colour" across different
   things next to a look-alike colour, with nobody showing first. M11 Stage 2 (not on his phone) fixes part of this (the host shows
   first, hands-on sorting) but keeps adult wording, traps on the first screen, spoken explanations as hints, no story and no laugh.
6. **She is 2, under the app's 3 to 8 range.** Nothing in the app is made for a child who is only just learning the colour words.

## 2. Goal

A child of 2 to 8 opens the app and wants to stay: every touch answers, every success is celebrated, the characters sound and act
alive, and the lesson is a small game with a reason, taught before it is asked. The learning goals and the diagnosis of mistakes stay.

Child-safety rules are unchanged: no ads, purchases, timers, streaks or guilt; rewards are for effort and discovery; no child audio
stored or sent; original characters only.

## 3. How we work

- **Colour Bay is the pilot.** Every step ships there first. The owner plays it with his daughter before it goes to the other lands.
- **A trial pack.** The pilot's content lives in the signed pack, so the owner signs a trial starter pack for the pilot (not published).
  Without it he cannot see the pilot on his phone.
- **M11 Stage 2 is not voiced yet.** Its lines will be reworded by the rules of this plan, so voicing waits until the pilot is approved.
  The approval list `docs/review/owner-approval-stage2.csv` is on hold for the same reason.
- Branch `delight`, cut from `lands-ux` once the size fixes are in.

## 4. Steps

### Step 0: finish the size fixes (M11, in progress)
The automatic size test, then the developer fixes what it lists. Everything in this plan is drawn to those minimum sizes.

### Step 1: sound and "juice" for every activity (whole app, no content change, no cost)
- An original sound kit made by a script in the repo: touch, pick-up, drop, six right-answer chimes, a soft try-again (never a buzzer),
  hint, counting notes that rise, lesson-end fanfare, sticker. Loudness targets, ducking under the voice, no sound spam.
- A light vibration on touches and right answers. A parent switch for sound effects, music and vibration, and a calm mode.
- One feedback layer shared by every template: the tile squashes under the finger; on a right answer a burst of confetti in the room's
  own shapes, a spark that flies to the progress trail, the companion really jumps, the stage answers. About one second, never blocks
  the next touch. Reduced-motion versions.
- Three sizes of celebration: S every right answer, M the end of a lesson, L a milestone.

### Step 2: the lesson-end party
The text card is replaced by a party in the room: host and companion jump in, confetti falls, the child taps the sticker into the book,
every tap makes something happen.

### Step 3: Colour Bay remade (content and a youngest stage)
- **A youngest stage for under-3s (P0)**, set by the birthday: two choices of 112 pt, nothing counts as wrong, one tap in place of a
  drag, three items a visit, straight from the map into play. No placement, no mastery, no revisits. Two to three "first steps" lessons
  per land.
- **Goni is the game.** He stays on screen, wants a colour, names it before he asks, and turns the colour the child finds. Three pilot
  lessons: "Goni wants red", "Two baskets", "Where is red?". One colour at a time; red, blue, yellow, green first; look-alike colours
  and "same colour on a different thing" move later. Mistakes are still diagnosed from age 3, after a first success.
- **Prompts as invitations** in a child's words, right-answer lines that celebrate and vary, hints that show, not explain.
- **Goni's paint pots:** a corner with no questions. Tap a pot, Goni turns that colour and says its name. Records no progress.
- 11 new drawings for the pilot.

### Step 4: voices that sound alive (the owner runs every paid command)
- A small audition (about 1,300 characters) the owner runs and listens to: the current voices with direction per kind of line (greeting,
  question, cheer, comfort) against the present flat read.
- A bank of 10 to 12 rotating cheers and reactions (giggle, wow, oops) per narrator; a voice for Goni; hero-son made equally loud.
- If generated Hebrew still sounds flat: about 30 key lines recorded by an adult in the owner's family, kept local.

### Step 5: the owner tries the pilot
A trial pack signed by the owner, the build on his phone, and an observation checklist (does she laugh, repeat, ask for more, how long
before she leaves). His verdict decides what changes before the roll-out.

### Step 6: roll-out
The other five lands and the three language courses by the 14 authoring rules of the teaching notes; a map where every tap does
something; emotion poses for the characters (about 157 drawings in all); a tune per land and host, a home theme, ambience. Then one
QA pass, the security review, the owner's approval of the lines, signing and voicing, once.

## 5. Acceptance criteria (pilot)

1. Every touch on a tile, object, map item or room item gives a visible and audible answer within 100 ms.
2. Every right answer gives the S celebration; a finished lesson gives the party; neither blocks a touch.
3. A child under 3 reaches something that reacts within 10 seconds of opening the app, with no reading and no drag.
4. In the P0 stage nothing is recorded as wrong and no placement runs.
5. In Colour Bay's first three lessons every word asked was shown and named first, one colour at a time.
6. No praise line repeats twice in a row; there are at least ten cheers per narrator.
7. Sound effects, music and vibration each have a parent switch; calm mode works; reduced motion is respected.
8. All sizes pass the automatic size test on the four boards.
9. No new package without the owner's OK; no secret, no child audio, no personal data to a vendor; nothing paid run by Claude.
10. The owner's own check with his children, by the observation checklist.

## 6. Cost

- Sound kit, music, drawings, code: no money.
- Audition: about 1,300 characters. Cheers bank: about 60 clips. Both small.
- A full re-voice after the owner picks a direction: about 12 to 20 USD (the sound director's estimate; the tool prints the real
  number before anything is spent).
