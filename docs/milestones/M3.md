## 1. Goal and "done when"

From PLAN.md, unchanged:

- Skill graph, mastery and memory model, misconception evidence, representation switching, session builder, and the simulator.
- **Done when:** the simulator finds planted gaps within 3 probes in at least 90% of cases; the engine imports no UI code.

M3 builds the brain, not the screens. It is a pure Dart library that reads the `attempt` rows M2 already writes, works out what each child knows, what they have forgotten, which misunderstanding they have and what to play next, and a simulator that proves the diagnosis works on thousands of invented children. The home map does not start using it yet (the starter pack has five activities; a session needs hundreds): M3 only shows its output on the dev page. Characters, voice and the tutor stay in M4–M6.

## 2. How we verify

Everything runs on this PC. The engine has no clock, no database and no randomness of its own (time and a seeded random generator are passed in), so every rule is a plain function that a test can call. Two things make the proof strong:

1. **Replay.** Every derived state is recomputed from the attempt log by one function, so a test can feed a written story ("the child answers 4 for 3, twice") and check each number.
2. **The simulator.** Invented children with planted gaps play against the real engine; the acceptance number is measured over thousands of them with a fixed seed, in CI, and printed as a table by `dart run tool/simulate.dart`.

## 4. Tasks, in order

### Phase A — Types, the skill graph and the replay (`app/lib/engine/`)

- **A1.** Plain types: `SkillNode` (id, prerequisites with weights, core, representations, tags, mastery requirements), `ItemMeta` (activity id, skills, purpose, check type, representation, variant group, difficulty, options count), `AttemptEvent` (what `attempt` holds), `EngineParams`.
- **A2.** `SkillGraph`: built from the skill and activity documents of the installed packs; refuses cycles; reports orphan skills and skills with no item in some representation (the CI checks of tech.md §7.1 become a content test on the starter pack).
- **A3.** `Engine.replay(events, now)` returns the whole state (below). Order of events never matters beyond their timestamps; replaying twice gives identical state.
- **Check:** graph tests (cycle, orphan, depth, weakest-prerequisite ranking); replay is deterministic; the engine files import nothing outside `dart:` and `engine/` (a test).

### Phase B — Mastery (BKT) and memory (FSRS-style)

- **B1.** Per-skill mastery estimate: BKT with the priors of curriculum.md §10.2, evidence weighted by hint credit (1.0, 0.8, 0.5, 0.0), "unsure" at 0.3 as incorrect, guess 1/k for k options, and 2-option items counting for at most half of the success evidence.
- **B2.** Status per skill: not started, ready, learning, fresh mastery (0.95, 4 successes over 2 days, 6 and 2 representations and 2 checks for core skills), durable mastery (a passed delayed check 7 or more days later on a different item of the variant group), needs revisit, gap.
- **B3.** Retention: stability and difficulty per skill, `R(t) = (1 + (19/81)·t/S)^(-0.5)`, next check when R reaches the target (1 to 90 days), stability up on a passed check (more when R was low), reset on a failed one.
- **Check:** hand-computed tables for 10 stories; R equals 0.9 at t = S; the status never goes back without a failed check; "nothing is ever removed" (a revisit never lowers a past mastery date).

### Phase C — Misconceptions, diagnostic walk, gaps

- **C1.** Evidence: `score = 0.8·score + strength` per tag, strength 1.0 for a tap; active at 1.5 across 2 or more different items; resolved after 3 diagnostic items that could have shown it and did not; correct answers on such items decay the score.
- **C2.** Diagnostic walk: starts after 2 failed attempts on a skill or an active tag; ranks prerequisites by weight × (1 − mastery), stale retention counting as weaker; one probe at a time, depth at most 3, at most 3 probes per session; a probe that fails recurses, one that passes tries the next; all passing means the gap is in the skill itself. Result: a `gap` record.
- **C3.** Repair and return: repair items are chosen in a representation other than the one that failed; after repair the builder offers an item whose `contradicts` is the tag (or any item that could have shown it); a pass clears the tag and the gap.
- **Check:** the three examples of curriculum.md §5.4 and PLAN.md ("52 − 27") as written stories; a slip does not make a misconception; unsure creates no tag.

### Phase D — Representations

- **D1.** Per child and skill family: tries and successes per representation; after 2 errors or a level-3 hint the next item uses another representation, picked by Thompson sampling with the curriculum's priors (concrete first in Band A) and a seeded random generator; the reward is success on the next two items.
- **Check:** with a fixed seed the bandit converges to the representation that really works for a simulated child within 40 trials in at least 90% of runs; it never picks the one that just failed.

### Phase E — The session builder

- **E1.** `SessionBuilder.next(state, band, elapsed, history)` returns the next item and why, following the table of PLAN.md (warm-up, repair up to 25%, focus 35–55%, revisit 15–20%, delight, wind-down), the limits (2 new skills a session, unlock at 0.8), the stuck rules, "end on success" (the last item at p of 0.85 or more), variety (revisits use another item of the variant group), and no repeat of the same item after a struggle.
- **E2.** Item choice by Elo-style difficulty aiming at the target success band (0.80–0.90 in Band A, 0.75–0.85 in B and C).
- **E3.** Database version 3: the `session` table and `attempt.session_id`, with a migration test from version 2.
- **Check:** 20 scripted sessions per band against a small invented catalogue; the proportions fall inside the table; a session always ends, always on a success, and never after the parent's limit.

### Phase F — The simulator

- **F1.** `SimChild` (true knowledge per skill, slip, guess, learning rate, forgetting, misconceptions it falls into) and `tool/simulate.dart`, which plays N children against the real engine and prints: gaps planted, found within 1, 2 and 3 probes, wrongly found (false gaps), items to find, sessions to repair, and how often a tag was resolved.
- **F2.** A CI test runs 1,000 children with a fixed seed and asserts the acceptance number (at least 90% found within 3 probes, false gaps under 5%).
- **Check:** the table is part of the report; if the number is short I change the engine (ranking, probe size, tag shortcuts) and show you the table before and after, not the target.

### Phase G — Proof and report

- **G1.** Dev page: "Engine state" for a child (skill statuses, active tags, gaps, next check dates) read from the real `attempt` rows, so after you play the butterflies lesson on your phone you can see what the engine concluded.
- **G2.** The starter pack rebuilt (skills with `core`/`masteryRequires`), `PLAN.md` and `PROGRESS.md` updated, report in the standard form.

## 9. Risks and unknowns

- **The 90% number.** Guessing among 3 options hides gaps; Q5 and the tag shortcut are the levers. If the simulator says 85%, I report 85% and what I changed, not a tuned test.
- **Invented children are not real children.** The simulator proves the logic, not the pedagogy; real calibration needs the beta (curriculum.md §14).
- **Starting values.** Every number comes from the curriculum or tech docs; none has been tested on a child yet.
- **A small catalogue.** Until M9 there are five activities, so the builder is proven on invented catalogues, not on the real content.

**Read this with the assumptions in mind.** The invented child has a planted gap one to three levels below the skill it fails (the depth the walk covers). "History" is how many skills the engine has real answers for: where the child knows a skill it did well, where it does not it did badly, and elsewhere the skill was only assumed (0.85). With no history the engine cannot tell which prerequisite is missing, so it finds the gap about half the time and names a wrong skill about a quarter of the time; the real app will have history, but this is the number that depends most on how children actually play. Probes assume the authors provide at least one **open** probe item (give, drag, trace: nothing to guess) for each skill that can be a gap; with only three-option probes the same search needs about 16 items and a Band A session (14 items) ends before it finishes (70% in the same test). That is now an authoring rule for M9.

What now exists, all in `app/lib/engine/` (pure Dart): types and `EngineParams` (every number tunable, JSON in and out), the skill graph (cycles refused, orphans reported), mastery (BKT with hint credit, "not sure" at 0.3, the two-option cap), memory (FSRS-style stability and retrievability, checks due when the chance of remembering falls to 90%), statuses (ready, learning, fresh mastery, durable mastery after a delayed check on a different item, needs a revisit, gap; nothing the child earned is ever removed), misconception evidence (`score = 0.8·score + 1`, active at 1.5 across two items, resolved after three diagnostic items), the gap search and repair tracking, the representation chooser (Thompson sampling, never the way that just failed), the session builder (warm-up, repair up to 25%, focus, revisits by memory, delight, the stuck rule, an end on an easy success), and the simulator. Outside the engine: `content/engine_adapter.dart` (pack documents and `attempt` rows to engine input), database version 3 (`session` table and `attempt.session_id`; the lesson now writes one session per visit, ended `finished` or `home`), the optional skill and activity fields (`core`, `masteryRequires`, `contradicts`), and the dev page's "Show what the engine knows" button.

### Plan drift (PLAN.md is updated in the same commit)

1. **Probe rule (Q5).** The plan said one item plus a confirming one. The simulator showed that is too easy to pass by guessing (11% at three choices). A probe now needs enough right answers that guessing alone passes it less than 2 times in 100 (four at three choices, three at four, one for an open item) and fails after two wrong answers (one for an open item). A wrong answer in a guessable item is a slip as often as not; this took false gaps from 7% to 1.5%.
2. **Reach.** The walk may probe up to three levels below the failing skill, not only its direct prerequisites; among nearly equally suspect skills it probes the lowest; a passed probe, and any skill the child has just shown it can do, clears everything below it. This assumes that knowing a skill implies its prerequisites.
3. **No gap when nothing is missing.** When every prerequisite passes, the search closes without a gap record (the skill is simply still being learned), instead of "the gap is in the skill itself". Searches always end.
4. **Q6 refined** as above: gaps one to three levels down, history on 80% of skills as the standard scenario, the other scenarios reported.
5. Free-play ("delight") items change nothing in the estimates. A skill in focus gets 4 to 5 items per session, so about two skills are in focus.
6. The lesson writes a session per visit (the plan only added the table).
7. The app version is now 0.2.0 (the new optional fields need it, so an M2 app keeps such a pack hidden) and the starter pack is 0.2.0, rebuilt and signed with the same key.
8. **Weights from memory.** The FSRS weights are the FSRS 4.5 defaults as I remember them; I could not check them against the reference here. Tests check the properties (0.9 at one stability, stability grows with passed checks and falls on a failure), not the numbers. They are starting values to refit.
9. Item difficulty uses the authored difficulty with a logistic curve (an Elo-style stand-in); there is no calibration until there are children's answers.
10. The starter pack has no probe items, so it cannot be probed yet (a test says so).
