# The Hebrew course: syllabus (scope and sequence)

Owner of this file: hebrew-language-teacher. Status: **draft for the owner's OK** (2026-11-03). Nothing here is built.
Evidence and sources: `docs/research/hebrew-course-2026-11-03.md` (cited below as **R §n**). Templates: `templates.md`. Voice and letter shapes: `voice-brief.md`.

**What this course is.** The Hebrew course teaches Hebrew as a language and its reading and writing, for any child aged 3 to 8 at any level. It runs from a child who has never noticed a letter to a child who reads and writes short pointed texts and the first unpointed words. It is **its own course** and has nothing to do with the app's Hebrew interface (owner decision 4, `docs/languages/README.md`): no other land depends on it, and it depends on no other land.

**The Hebrew words and syllables in this file were edited by the hebrew-text-editor on 2026-11-03: nikud, stress, spelling and fit for a child are checked, and the status is `text-ready`** (waiting for the owner's approval; the decisions and the changes are in `docs/agents/hebrew-notes.md` §8). New Hebrew written later stays `"review": "needs-editor"` until the editor has read it. Example words are chosen to be decodable and picturable; their pointing follows the app's standard (`docs/agents/hebrew-notes.md` §2.4: full nikud on the defective spelling, the Academy's spelling for pointed text). Sentences (S11), instructions, hints and praise are not written yet.

## 0. How to read this file

- **Skill ids:** `HEB-<band>-<nn>` (bands A 3–4, B 5–6, C 7–8, as in `docs/PLAN.md`). A band is a label for the usual age. A child moves by mastery, never by age or date (`docs/curriculum.md` §4.1).
- **Strands:** OL oral language (listening, vocabulary, morphology, story); PA phonological awareness; PK print and letter knowledge; DEC decoding and reading; WR writing (handwriting and spelling).
- **The code spine:** letters and vowel signs come in twelve steps S1–S12 (§3). A DEC or WR skill "for step Sn" uses only the letters and signs of S1…Sn.
- **Mastery:** the standard evidence SE of `docs/curriculum.md` §4.1 (BKT ≥ 0.95, independent correct answers over ≥ 2 days, at least two representations, one understanding check; durable after a delayed check ≥ 7 days later). The "Mastery seen" column names the two representations and the check for each skill. Every piece of evidence is a **tap, drag or trace**. A spoken answer or reading aloud never counts against a child, and does not count for mastery either (R §12).
- **References:** **MoE-RW** = the Ministry's reading-and-writing goals (2026), with the stage (1 = ages 3–4, 2 = 4–5, 3 = 5–6, 4 = grade 1, 5 = grade 2); **MoE-CC** = the Ministry's communicative-competence goals (2026); **ML** = the grade 1 policy letter תשפ"ו. The research is cited by author (full references in R).
- **Distractor tags** (`M-HEB-…`) are defined in §5. Every item carries at least two tagged distractors (`lesson-review` §2).

## 2. The phonological-awareness ladder (and why this order)

Hebrew speakers hear a syllable as **body + coda** (CV + C), not onset + rime (Share and Blum 2005, R §4). The Ministry's gan stages climb the same way (MoE-RW 2–3). So the ladder is:

1. **Rhyme:** enjoy, complete, judge (A-06, A-07).
2. **Syllables:** count, blend (A-08, A-09), isolate the first one (B-07), delete one (B-08).
   - Words go in this order: open syllables only (דֻּבִּי, מַתָּנָה), then open and closed (חָ–לָב, חֻלְ–צָה), then a cluster (גְּלִי–דָה), then closed only (סַפְ–סָל) (MoE-RW 2).
3. **The opening CV unit** (B-09): תּוּ in תּוּת, מִ in מִבְרֶשֶׁת.
4. **The final consonant** (B-10): ones that can be stretched first (ל, שׁ, ר, מ, ס, ח), stops later.
5. **The initial consonant** (B-11): words that open with a cluster first (גְּלִידָה, שְׂמִיכָה), where the CV need not be split, then CV-initial words (בֻּבָּה), and continuants (שׁ ז ס ל ר ח מ נ) before stops (MoE-RW 3).
6. **Blend and segment** CV units and consonants (B-12, B-13), with tokens, right to left.

From step 3 on, every phonological activity can show the letters (Ehri et al. 2001: phonemic awareness works best with letters). From Band C on, phonological work lives inside reading and spelling.

## 3. The code spine: letters and vowel signs in twelve steps

**Why this order** (R §5; owner decisions 1 and 2):
- **Vowels:** a → i → o → u → e → shva → hatafs. The Ministry says the order "is not binding" and that most methods open with /a/. a has two signs and one sound, and its final ָה starts the vowel letters. i is one dot. o and u share ו and are contrasted on purpose. e (two signs, one sound) comes when three vowels are secure. Shva at the start of a word, and the hatafs, are the hardest for letter-by-letter reading.
- **Letters:** in six groups, with four aims:
  - each group makes real, picturable words with the vowels taught so far;
  - continuants come early;
  - confusable letters are apart in time and then contrasted on purpose (ב S1 / כ S4; ר S1 / ד S2; ת S1 / ה S4 / ח S5; ן S2 / ו S4 / ז S6; ם S1 / ס S2; ע S5 / צ S6);
  - one sound per letter first, so בּ כּ פּ (b, k, p) and שׁ come first, and ב כ פ (v, χ, f) and שׂ (s) are their own step, S7.
- **A child's own name** comes before all of this, as a doorway (A-11, B-27). Its letters are learnt whatever group they belong to.

**Notes on the steps.**
- The CV unit is the unit a child reads and hears. Each step opens with its CV units (a letter with each vowel taught, the Ministry's "גלגל סימני ניקוד": בָּ בִּ בּוֹ…), then two-unit words, then longer ones (MoE-RW 4: each sign alone first, then combined).
- A step can start before every letter of its group is mastered: decoding the first two or three letters of a group may begin once their sounds are known.
- ה is used from S1 only as part of the final ָה ("one package", ML appendix). It is taught as a consonant in S4. A final א after a vowel (אַבָּא, בָּא, הִיא) is read the same way from S1: it makes no sound and ends the word. A silent א inside a word (רֹאשׁ) is taught in S7.
- **Checked by script:** every word in S1–S8 uses only the letters and signs of its step or earlier (138 words; the check marks ב כ פ without a dagesh as S7, a shva on the first letter as S8, and so on). Four words were moved after the first run (סָבָא, קֻפְסָה and זָהָב have a soft ב or פ; חוּט has ט). **Re-run by the editor on 2026-11-03 after the changes: 143 words in S1–S8 (13 CV units besides), none needs a later step** (details, the script and what changed: `hebrew-notes.md` §8.1). S9–S12 are read by hand: 19 + 17 + 17 words.
- Final forms: ם in S1 and ן in S2, each with its letter. ץ in S6. ך and ף in S7, because their usual sound is the soft one (ךְ = χ, ף = f).
- Dagesh chazak (doubling: לָמָּה, אִמָּא) changes no sound in modern Hebrew. The child is told "the dot in מּ does not change the sound". Only in ב כ פ does it change the sound (S7).
- At least **8 picturable words per step** are needed for the activities. **S3 is settled (editor, 2026-11-03): it stays its own short step, with 8 picturable words** (יַלְדָּה, תַּלְמִיד, נִרְדָּם, מִדְבָּר, תַּרְמִיל, סַנְדָּל, בִּנְיָן, לַיְלָה: four were found with S1–S2 letters only). It is not merged into S4: that step already brings four letters and a vowel. The silent shva then comes back in S5–S8 words (שֻׁלְחָן, אֶצְבַּע, שִׂמְלָה, סַבְתָּא, קֻפְסָה, צְפַרְדֵּעַ), which the activities use as review. The lists above give the candidates. The pictures are the design-director's, drawn from real references (`docs/art/`).

## 4. Letter facts the course teaches (and the voice must say)

- **The 22 letters, in alphabet order**, with name, sound(s) and step: `voice-brief.md` §2 (the pronunciation table).
- **Final forms:** ך ם ן ף ץ, "the same letter at the end of a word, with a long leg or closed". They are taught as a pair with their letter (B-17, C-20).
- **Dagesh in בכפ:** בּ b / ב v, כּ k / כ χ, פּ p / פ f. At the start of a word the letter usually has the dot (בַּיִת, כֶּלֶב, פִּיל). After a vowel it usually does not (כּוֹכָב, סֵפֶר). The rule is shown to children only as a pattern: "the dot makes the hard sound".
- **Shin and sin:** שׁ (dot on the right) is sh, שׂ (dot on the left) is s.
- **אהו"י:** each is a consonant and also, in some words, part of a vowel: ָה/ֶה at the end, וֹ וּ, ִי ֵי. א and ע make no sound of their own in modern Hebrew; they carry the vowel. These are named goals (MoE-RW 4).
- **Homophones**, for spelling (C-22): ט/ת, כּ/ק, ח/כ, ב/ו, א/ה/ע, ס/שׂ (MoE-RW 4).

## 7. The writing sequence (one place)

1. **Pre-writing strokes** (A-14): down, right to left, arch, circle. Scribble-writing in play, unscored.
2. **Print letters** (B-24 → B-25 → B-26): trace → copy → write from memory, per letter group, finger on the screen. The app judges where the stroke starts, its direction and its shape. Feedback comes at the end of a stroke, never during it (Patchan and Puranik 2016).
3. **Own name** (B-27), on the device only.
4. **Phonetic words with tiles** (B-28): first and last sound → all consonants. Partial and homophone spellings are "on the way".
5. **Full phonetic spelling** (C-19), with vowel letters, then the **final forms** (C-20).
6. **Conventional spelling** of frequent words and function letters (C-21), then **homophone letters by word family** (C-22).
7. **Sentences:** build (C-23), then their own (C-24).
8. **Script letters** (C-25 → C-26 → C-27): recognise, then trace and copy by direction group, then write from memory.
9. **Paper:** every handwriting skill has a parent co-play card for pencil and paper (curriculum §12.2). Writing on paper is where handwriting is really learnt (R §8).

## 8. Oral language: how a unit is built

- **A story unit** is one original short story (Band A: 4–6 pages, ≤ 8 words per sentence; Band B: 6–8 pages; Band C: a text the child partly reads). It has 3–5 target words, 2–3 dialogic pauses with picture questions, and one parent co-play card with an open question and a "your own story" prompt (Whitehurst et al. 1988; Mol et al. 2009).
- **Target words** come back in three new contexts within the unit and in later retrieval (curriculum §1 principle 8).
- **Morphology by ear** (A-05, B-03, C-16, C-17) uses pictures, never grammar terms with the child.
- **Rhymes and songs** are original (owner decision 12). They carry A-06/A-07 and the letter-name song.
- All stories, rhymes and questions are **new Hebrew**, so they are drafts for the hebrew-text-editor and need pronunciation specs.

## 9. Placement entry points

Two strands are placed separately (R §11): **L** = oral language and phonological awareness, **C** = the code (letters, reading, writing). The curriculum's staircase applies (§10.1: ≤ 8 items, 2 right up / 1 wrong down, start one unit low, assumed skills verified within 2 weeks, no result shown to the child). Every item is a tap or drag.

| Entry | The child can… (what the probe checks) | Starts at |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | not yet: rhyme or syllable clapping | A-01, A-06, A-08 (L); A-10 (C) |
| P1 | rhyme and clap syllables; tells letters from other marks | B-07 (L); A-11, B-14 S1 (C) |
| P2 | finds first and last sounds; knows some letter names | B-09/B-10 (L); B-14/B-15 at the first unknown group (C) |
| P3 | knows most letter sounds; reads CV units with a | B-20 (C) |
| P4 | reads words with a and i, slowly | C-01 (C), after a quick check of B-23 |
| P5 | reads pointed words with all vowels | C-06 (C); C-09 next |
| P6 | reads pointed sentences and short texts | C-11 (C); C-13 offered |

- **The probes, in order:** rhyme pick → syllable count → first sound pick → letter name (hear → tap) → letter sound → read a CV unit → read a word → picture → read a sentence → picture.
- **Reading** is placed only by "read, then tap the picture", with a look-alike word and a sound-alike word among the choices.
- A parent may answer one question ("does the child read pointed words?") that sets **where the staircase starts**, never the result.
- **The L strand of a P4+ child** still starts at the lowest unmastered oral-language skill of their age band, so a child who reads early does not miss vocabulary and story work.

## 10. Session shape and review

- **Session length and items** per band as in PLAN.md (§1 above).
- **A Hebrew session** mixes one code activity (PA, letters or decoding), one listening or story activity, and one writing activity in Bands B and C. It opens with retrieval of earlier letters or words (curriculum §1 principle 8).
- **New letters per week:** no calendar. The engine moves on when a group is mastered. As a guide, the Ministry finishes all letters and signs in about six months of daily small-group teaching (ML principle 13). An app used a few times a week will take longer, and that is fine.
- **Revisits:** the curriculum's FSRS schedule. Confusable pairs are revisited together.
- **Stuck:** after the hint ladder and a second representation (CV-first ↔ word-first, ML), a child who still struggles gets the prerequisite repair. After repeated repairs on the same code skill, the parent area shows a gentle note suggesting a talk with the gan teacher or the school. It gives no label (R §12).
