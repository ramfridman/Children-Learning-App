## 1. How to read this file

- **Stages 1-7** are the content path every child walks, in order, from the first word to short texts (research §5.1):
  1 First words · 2 Talking and sound play · 3 Sounds and letters · 4 Reading words · 5 More sounds · 6 Reading sentences · 7 Reading and writing short texts.
- **Skill ids** `ENG-<band>-<nn>` carry the band where the skill is usually first taught (A 3-4, B 5-6, C 7-8). A child is placed by mastery, not by age: a 7-year-old who never heard English starts at `ENG-A-01` with Band C presentation (§6).
- **Strands:** LIS listening and vocabulary · SPK speaking and chunks · PA phonological awareness and the new English sounds · PH phonics (letters, sounds, decoding) · RD reading for meaning · WR writing (letter formation, spelling, sentences).
- **Mastery** follows `docs/curriculum.md` §10.2 (fresh mastery: P(L) ≥ 0.95, at least 4 successes over at least 2 days, at least 2 representations, an understanding check; durable mastery after a delayed check at least 7 days later with a different item). "Mastery is seen" says what the app counts. 2-choice items count at most 50% of the evidence.
- **Speaking skills** (SPK) cannot be scored today: the recogniser is Hebrew only, and child speech is unreliable. They are **practised**, not machine-mastered: the child echoes, then shows understanding by a tap; a parent may confirm "said it" in co-play (as in T24). Their progress shows as "practised" to the parent until owner decision 4 (§9).
- **Errors**: the tags in the last column are defined in §8 (typical errors of Hebrew speakers).
- **Templates**: `T01`-`T25` are the app's catalogue (`docs/curriculum.md` §6.2); `LT01`-`LT11` are new language templates proposed in `templates.md`.

## 3. The phonics order (stages 3-6)

Systematic synthetic phonics adapted for Hebrew speakers (research §5.3). Sounds are General American (pending owner decision 1; §3.5 gives the British changes). Lowercase first. A group is mastered when its sounds are known both ways and its words are read and built; one group at a time; earlier groups are revisited in every session's warm-up.

### 3.1 Letter-sound groups

### 3.2 Blending and segmenting (how a word is read and built)
1. **Oral first** (B-14, B-15): the robot says the sounds, the child finds the word; then the child splits a word into sounds with tokens. No letters.
2. **Sound buttons** (B-18 onward): each letter of a word has a dot (a bar under a digraph); the child slides a finger **left to right**, each sound plays, then the whole word plays. Continuous sounds (s, m, n, f, l, v, z, sh, th) are stretched into the next sound ("sssaaat"), stops are clipped ([MoE-PF]: "read each phoneme aloud correctly, blending").
3. **Build** (B-20 onward): hear the word, put the letters in left-to-right slots; the slots light one by one as the sounds are said.
4. **Read for meaning**: the word or sentence is matched to a picture; this is what the engine counts.
5. **Decodable only**: a word is used for reading only when all its graphemes are taught, or it is a taught tricky word (lesson-review §3).

### 3.3 Tricky words (common words with an unusual part)
Taught as whole words, with the tricky part pointed out ("the 'o' in 'to' says /u/").

| Set | Stage | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 (B-21) | I, a, the, to, is |
| 2 | 5 (C-11) | he, she, we, me, be, my (decodable once group 7 is taught), you, they, was, said, are, of, do, no, go, so |
| 3 | 6 (C-11) | what, where, here, there, one, two, come, some, have, like, love, little, all, call |
Notes: "a" and "the" are taught with their weak forms /ə/, /ðə/ (and /ði/ before a vowel). "is, has, his, as" end in /z/.

### 3.4 Letter names and the alphabet
Letter **sounds** first; letter **names** from stage 3 through the alphabet song (B-22), then in Band C for spelling a name and for alphabetical order (T05). The child is never asked to read by names. Capitals from stage 5 (C-13).

### 3.5 If the owner chooses British English
Spelling changes per §9. Sounds change in: short o (/ɒ/ in hot), r-controlled vowels (car /kɑː/, fork /fɔːk/, her /hɜː/: the r is silent), "ee" /iː/, "oo" /uː/, "oa" /əʊ/, and the a in words like "bath, grass" (/ɑː/; avoided in group 3 then). The groups and their order stay.

**Child safety:** the Ministry's personal-information chunks ([MoE-PreA1]: name, age, address) are taught only through characters and pictures. ### 4.3 How a word is taught (every word, every band)
1. Heard with its picture and, where possible, an action (never translated first).
2. Heard again in a chunk ("It's a cat."), then in a song or story.
3. Chosen among 2-3 pictures (listening), then among 3-4 with a sound or meaning neighbour (distractor with a tag).
4. Echoed (practised); in later stages read and built.
5. Revisited by the spaced schedule (1 → 3 → 7 → 21 → 60 days, [Curr]); at least 6 meaningful encounters before a check of durable mastery.
Hint ladder per item: H1 replay slower with the picture glowing; H2 a different representation (the action shown, or the Hebrew gloss for an abstract word); H3 a worked example ("show me") where the friend picks and says it.

### 6.2 The oral gate (before letters)
A Band B or C child starts the literacy strand when **(a)** the stage 1-2 listening core is mastered (A-01 to A-11, A-16, B-01, B-02, B-05, B-06: about 100 words and the stage 1-2 chunks understood) and **(b)** B-12, B-13 and B-14 onset-rime are mastered. For Band C, the oral core may be fresh mastery rather than durable, so a grade-3 beginner reaches letters in about 20-30 sessions, close to the Ministry's 3-4 months ([MoE-PF]). Band A never opens the literacy strand. A parent can keep the course "oral only" (owner decision 2).

### 6.3 The placement game
`docs/curriculum.md` §10.1 adapted: a "visit to the English island" of up to 10 items, listening first (pictures), then chunks; letter and reading items appear only for Band B-C children whose parent ticked "knows some English letters". Two right answers move up, one miss moves down; stop after 2 reversals or 10 items; start one level below the highest passed. Skipped skills are "assumed" (prior 0.85) and checked by interleaved retrieval in the first two weeks. Every placement item gets warm feedback; no result is shown to the child. The parent sees "we start here" and can move it one level.

## 7. Sessions and Hebrew support

### 7.1 Session shape (per `docs/curriculum.md` §10.3, the English defaults)
| Band | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| A | 6-8 min | hello song (LT02) → 2 retrieval items → 1 new word set (3-4 words, T01/LT01) → echo (LT04) → song or story (LT02/LT03) → goodbye |
| B | 10-12 min | hello routine → 3 retrieval items → one new item of one strand (words or sounds or letters) → practice in 2 representations → a chunk role-play or a song → goodbye |
| C | 12-15 min | routine chunk → 4 retrieval items (mixed strands) → new phonics or reading skill → build or write → read (sentence or story) → goodbye |
At most 2 new skills per session; never a new letter group and a new word set together in Band B.

### 7.2 Hebrew around the English ("Hebrew frame, English target")
- The **instruction** of an item is Hebrew at first ("Hebrew frame", hebrew-text-editor), the **target** is English, voiced as separate clips (`voice-brief.md` §3).
- **English classroom chunks** replace the Hebrew instruction step by step (with the same icon, and the Hebrew still on the replay button): Listen! (stage 1) · Look! · Touch the ___. · Find the ___. · What's this? (stage 2) · Read. · Build the word. · Write. (stage 3-4) · Read the sentence. · Put the words in order. (stage 6).
- **Hebrew glosses** only at hint level 2, or for an abstract word, after the English was heard twice.
- **Explicit contrasts** in Hebrew, once each, when a skill starts: English goes left to right (B-18); English has big and small letters (C-13); this sound is not in Hebrew (B-11, C-02, C-03), with a mouth picture.
- **Praise and "not yet"** stay in Hebrew from the shared feedback lines (`docs/curriculum.md` §9); a few English praise chunks ("Well done!", "Great!") are taught as words.

## 9. Decisions for the owner (from this syllabus)

1. **American or British English.** **Recommendation: American spelling and a General American voice** (research §5.9). Effect here: color, gray, mom, favorite, pants, cookie, candy; the r-controlled and short-o sounds in §3.1. If British: colour, grey, mum, favourite, trousers, biscuit, sweets, and §3.5.
2. **When reading starts.** Recommendation: Band A oral only; Band B letters after the oral gate (§6.2), with a parent "oral only" switch; Band C full literacy after a compressed oral period.
3. **Hebrew support.** Recommendation: §7.2, "Hebrew frame, English target".
4. **Speaking.** Recommendation: unscored echo and role-play now; an English on-device recogniser is a separate later decision.

The skill list, order and prerequisites need the curriculum-expert's check against the shared engine (prerequisites without cycles; at most 2 new skills per session) and the owner's OK before a milestone plan.
