## 1. What the course is

A course in Russian for **any** child aged 3 to 8 whose app language is Hebrew: listening, speaking, **reading and writing in Cyrillic**, from the first sound to reading and writing short texts. Lessons are taught in Hebrew by a voice that speaks Hebrew and Russian (owner's decision 6); everything the child is learning is heard in Russian.

|---|---|---|---|

## 2. The method in one page
1. **Meaning first.** New words come with a picture and an action, in short repeated routines, in Russian only (no Hebrew translation of the word in the voice; the picture is the meaning) [ASH][MOL][MUN].
2. **Sounds before letters** (the Elkonin–Zhurova sound-analytic method) [ZHU][NRP][RAK]: the child stretches each sound of a word, lays chips in a row of boxes (one per sound), then colours them: **red vowel, blue hard consonant, green soft consonant**; finds the stressed vowel; counts syllables by vowels.
3. **Each letter is taught whole** (D16, frame §5.3), a few at a time (§3): its shape, its **sound** (in a key word with a picture; a stop is heard in a syllable), its **name** as a separate fact ("its name is эм, it says ммм", §3.1), and its writing (LS02 trace, then copy, then memory). Always both ways: hear a sound → find its letter; see a letter → choose its sound; hear a name → find the letter; see a letter → choose its name. **Blending always uses the sound, never the name** ("ма", not "эм-а"); the name-for-sound error has its own tag (M-RUS-NAME) and a NAME distractor in the letter items.
4. **Reading by the syllable merge** (слияние): the consonant "runs to" the vowel and they are said together (ммма → ма). The **vowel strip** in pairs (а–я, о–ё, у–ю, ы–и, э–е) teaches that the vowel letter tells whether the consonant before it is hard or soft [ZHU].
5. **Decodable words only**: a word appears for reading only when every letter in it has been taught (§4), and, for new learners, only when the child knows the word's meaning.
6. **Write to learn letters**: **trace → copy → write from memory** (D4; LS02 modes trace, copy and memory): trace with the full guide, then the start dot only; copy beside the model with no path in the box; then write from memory when the voice says the sound or the name [JAM][LON]. Mastery of the writing facet needs memory-mode evidence. Spell with **letter tiles** (the cut-out alphabet, LS06) before handwriting can [ZHU].
7. **Retrieval and spacing**: every session opens with earlier skills; revisits on the app's schedule (`docs/curriculum.md` §5.7, §10).
8. **Three tries, hints that change the help, never "wrong"** (`docs/curriculum.md` §9.2). An unclear spoken answer is never "wrong" and never counts against the child (§8).

## 3. Letter order (33 letters in 10 groups)

| Group | Letters | Why here | First syllables | Band |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | А У О М | Three clear vowels that exist in Hebrew; М can be stretched, so the first merge is easy to hear (ммма). Gives the first word, ма́ма. | ма мо му, ам ум ом | B |
| 2 | С Н Ы | Two frequent, stretchable consonants (с, н: 5th and 7th most frequent [NKRY]); ы early because it is new to Hebrew ears and needed with hard consonants (мы, сын). | са со су сы, на но ну ны | B |
| 3 | Л Р И | Frequent sonorants; И brings the **first soft consonants** (ли, ми, ни, си, ри) and the contrast with ы (мы / ми, лы / ли) while the hard row is fresh. И is separated from Н by one group (look-alike for children who know Latin N/H). | ла ло лу лы ли, ра … ри | B |
| 4 | К Т В | Frequent; К and Т are stops, met after the child can merge with stretchable sounds. В is the 9th most frequent letter. | ка ко ку, та то ту ты ти, ва во ву вы ви | B |
| 5 | Е П З | Е is the 2nd most frequent letter [NKRY]; it softens the consonant (лес, мел) and says [йэ] at the start of a word (ель). П is kept away from Н and И (print look-alikes). З after С (pair [с]/[з]). | пе, ле, ме, не, зе; па по пу пы пи | C (B for fast learners) |
| 6 | Д Г Я Ь | Д is three groups after В (shape confusion б/в/д kept apart). Я: its two jobs (я́ма, мя́со — taught with the vowel strip а–я). Ь: softness at the end of a word (конь, соль, пять). | да ду ды ди де, га го гу, ля ня мя ся тя | C |
| 7 | Б Ч Ш Й | Б two groups after В. Ч is always soft; ча, чу are written with а, у (spelling rule later). Ш is always hard; separated from Щ (group 10). Й four groups after И. | ба бо бу бы би, ча чу чи, ша шо шу ши, ай ой уй | C |
| 8 | Ж Ё Ю | Ж pairs with Ш (жи–ши); Ё always stressed (ёж, ёлка, мёд); Ю completes the pairs у–ю. Ж and Х (look-alike) are kept in different groups. | жа жо жу жи, лё нё, лю ню | C |
| 9 | Х Ц Э | Less frequent. Ц is always hard (цирк, огуре́ц); Э mostly at the start of a word (э́то, э́хо). | ха хо ху хи, ца цо цу цы | C |
| 10 | Щ Ф Ъ | The least frequent letters [NKRY]. Щ is always soft (щу́ка, щи); Ф (фото, флаг); Ъ separates (съел ↔ сел). | ща щу щи, фа фо фу | C |

Notes:
- The first 15 letters by corpus frequency (о е а и н т с р в л к м д п у) are all in groups 1–6 [NKRY].
- Capital and small forms are taught together (Аа). Most Cyrillic small letters are smaller copies of the capitals (к, м, н, т, п, ш), so the load is lighter than in Latin; the ones that differ (а, б, е, р, у) get their own matching items.
- Look-alike pairs taught in different groups: Н/И/П (1 group apart at least), Б/В/Д, Ш/Щ, И/Й, Ж/Х, З/Э.
- Sound pairs voiced/voiceless (б–п, в–ф, г–к, д–т, ж–ш, з–с) are compared once both letters are known (C-20).
- This is a hybrid of the Goretsky order (vowels first, then by frequency) and the Zhukova order (stretchable М, С first) [GOR]; no trial compares orders (research report §4.1).
- Each group is one **bundle** (frame §3.3.3): the group's letter skill and its tracing sub-skill open together (`B-18` with `B-24.G1`, `B-20` with `B-24.G2`, `B-21` with `B-24.G3`, `B-22` with `B-24.G4`, `C-01` with `B-24.G5` … `C-06` with `B-24.G10`), with no prerequisite between them, and count as one new skill in a visit. Copy and memory writing (`B-26.Gn`) follow as the next skill of the same group.

### 3.1 Letter names (taught with each group, D16)
The school names, as in Russian primers and dictionaries. Stress is marked for the voice where a name has more than one syllable. The name is heard as a separate fact after the sound: (Hebrew frame, draft, `"review": "needs-editor"`) `קוראים לה` + the name clip, then `והיא אומרת` + the sound clip.

| Group | Letter: name |
|---|---|
| 1 | А: а · У: у · О: о · М: эм |
| 2 | С: эс · Н: эн · Ы: ы |
| 3 | Л: эль · Р: эр · И: и |
| 4 | К: ка · Т: тэ · В: вэ |
| 5 | Е: е · П: пэ · З: зэ |
| 6 | Д: дэ · Г: гэ · Я: я · Ь: мя́гкий знак |
| 7 | Б: бэ · Ч: че · Ш: ша · Й: и кра́ткое |
| 8 | Ж: жэ · Ё: ё · Ю: ю |
| 9 | Х: ха · Ц: цэ · Э: э |
| 10 | Щ: ща · Ф: эф · Ъ: твёрдый знак |

- **Name = sound:** the vowel letters а, о, у, ы, э, и. е, ё, ю, я say their name ([йэ], [йо], [йу], [йа]) at the start of a word or after a vowel; after a consonant they are taught by what they do (they make it soft; C-01, C-02, C-04, C-07).
- **Name ≠ sound** (an explicit "its name is …, it says …" line, and a NAME-family distractor in their items, frame §5.3): the consonants whose name puts the sound **last** (эль, эм, эн, эр, эс, эф), the consonants whose name adds a vowel **after** the sound (бэ, вэ, гэ, дэ, жэ, зэ, пэ, тэ, цэ, ка, ха, ша, ща, че), and **й** (и кра́ткое, which says [й]).
- **No sound of their own:** ь and ъ are taught by what they do (ь softens: конь; ъ keeps apart: съел), never "read" by their names.
- эль is the form in most primers; эл is also correct and is accepted in the parent area. Alphabetical order is C-25.

Grammar is never explained to Band A–B; it is heard and used in fixed phrases with pictures. Band C names a few rules only where spelling needs them.

Voice rule (as for Hebrew, `docs/PLAN.md` › Speaking to the child): core lines never reveal the speaker's gender, so the teaching voice avoids the past tense about itself (я был / я была́) and adjectives about itself; present tense and imperatives are gender-neutral in Russian. Instructions address the child with ты in the Russian clips only where a Russian line is needed (Band C reading prompts); the Hebrew instruction carries the rest.

Scripted personal turns (D10, CHANGED; frame §5.2) are allowed in LS10 `personal` mode only: Как тебя́ зову́т? Ско́лько тебе́ лет? Что ты лю́бишь? Кто у тебя́ в семье́? The puppet answers first, there is always a "pass", the answer is never checked, stored, sent or used to choose content, and a parent switch turns them off. Где ты живёшь? (an address, a city, a gan or school) is never asked.

## 7. Routes and placement

### 7.1 Three routes
| | New learner | Heritage | Reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who | No Russian at home | Hears Russian at home; understands some or much; may speak little; usually does not read | Already reads Russian (any age) |
| Oral strands (LIS, VOC, GRM, SPK) | All of Band A, then B, C; slow pace, many repetitions; meaning by picture and action | Placement tests out what is understood; the route focuses on **speaking** (A-15, B-08, C-30), the grammar gaps (B-03, B-07, C-26, C-28) [MEIR][ROD][KDP20] and themes beyond home (B-01, C-29) | Oral strands placed separately (a reader may be heritage or not) |
| Sounds and letters (PHO, LET) | Start PHO when the child knows enough words to play with them (gate: A-12 and three themes mastered); letters from age 5 at the earliest by default | Start PHO at once with familiar words; letters as soon as B-12 is mastered | Placed by staircase; usually tests out |
| Reading (REA) | Words only after their meaning is known; sentences use only known chunks | Fast: syllable tables and words; texts with home vocabulary | Fluency and texts (C-08, C-12 → C-14) |
| Writing (WRI) | Tiles, tracing, memory; spelling rules late | Same; the spelling rules are where heritage speakers need most help (they write what they hear) | Spelling rules and dictation |
| Hebrew support | Every instruction in Hebrew; Russian target items; new word shown with picture | Hebrew instructions for every child in the first version (D12); a parent switch for Russian-first instructions may come later | As heritage |
| Stress marks on screen | Always in the stress skills (B-17, C-10, C-21); on new words in early reading (reading items and texts from B-23 on) | Only in the stress skills | Only in the stress skills |

The stress-mark defaults are approved (D15, frame Q-F4, 2026-11-03); a parent switch turns them on or off for the child. A mark is never placed on ё.

### 7.3 The placement game
- **Parent questionnaire** (parent area, enums only, nothing free-text, nothing sent to a vendor): Russian at home (no one / one parent / both / grandparents or others); the child understands (no / some / most); speaks (no / words / sentences); knows Russian letters (no / some / most); reads Russian (no / syllables / words / sentences). It chooses where each staircase **starts**, nothing more.
- **Five short staircases**, 4–8 items each, the app's rule (two right → up a level; one wrong → down; stop after 2 reversals or 8 items; place one unit below the highest level with ≥ 2/3 right) [GAG10 for what to sample]:
  1. Understanding: hear a word → tap its picture (4 pictures) → a chunk (Где мяч?) → a sentence (Ко́шка спит на сту́ле) → a grammar contrast (Я ви́жу ко́шку / ко́шка ви́дит меня́, picture choice).
  2. Sounds: rhyme → syllable count → first sound → hard or soft.
  3. Letters: hear a sound → tap the letter, rising through the groups.
  4. Reading: syllable → word → sentence, each matched to a picture.
  5. Spelling: build a heard word from tiles.
- Every placement item gets positive feedback; nothing is shown to the child; the parent sees "we start here" per strand and can move it one level. Speaking is not placed by the recogniser (frame §3.2: speech is never a placement probe); the parent's answer sets it.
- The placement game is LS13 (frame §3.2): a strand whose first two probes fail at its floor stops at once; the strands run across the first 2–3 visits, at most about 8 probe items in one visit; the questionnaire skips strands 3 to 5 for a child with no letters; the new-learner letters gate (D17: A-12 and three themes, and age 5 or more, with a parent override) is checked after placement.

## 8. Speaking: per-word feedback from a Russian recogniser (D1, CHANGED; frame §5.1)
The app understands Russian too, so speaking gets real feedback, per word, never per sound. Nothing of the child's voice is kept.

- **Where the microphone is used:** LS09 say-it (A-15 and every new word), the say step of LS10 (A-01, A-14, C-27, C-30), LS08 `echo` and `read_along` (B-08, C-13, C-14, C-30 and reading aloud).
- **Where it is never used:** placement; any scored listening, reading or spelling item (taps decide those); isolated sounds and syllables (sound work is always a tap); free speech in a story; a personal turn as a check (frame §5.2).
- **Conditions:** the parent turned voice on **for Russian**; the Russian model is installed and its hash verified; the microphone opens only on a tap and closes after a silence; recognition is streaming and on the device; no audio is stored, sent or played back; no transcript is kept; the result is an enum.
- **Matching, per word:** the expected set is the target, the variant forms I list for the item (for example мя́чик for мяч, ко́тик for кот), and the **foils**: the item's other options and the minimal-pair partner (лук / люк, мы́шка / ми́шка, рысь / рис, кот / кит). **Confident** = the target is at or above the Russian accept threshold and clear of every foil by the margin; for a chunk, every key word I mark is confident. **Unsure** = everything else, including a confidently heard foil and silence. There is no "wrong" outcome and no "did you say …?" question. The matcher normalises ё to е and drops stress marks; it judges words, so a reduced vowel costs nothing, and a misplaced stress is not judged (stress is taught by the listening items of B-17 and C-10).
- **Feedback** (Hebrew drafts, `"review": "needs-editor"`; the Russian word is its own target clip): **confident** → `שמעתי:` + the word clip (it praises the saying, not the child); **unsure** → `בּוֹאוּ נֹאמַר יַחַד.` (`text-ready` from the hebrew-text-editor on 2026-11-03; voice text `בואו נגיד יחד.`; `docs/agents/hebrew-notes.md` §8.8), the voice models the word, a choral pause, then the activity moves on. At most one more try, offered by a tap, never required; no retry loop; no score, no red mark, no sound-level correction.
- **Accent:** р said the Hebrew way (M-RUS-R) and other accent features are never judged. Hard / soft and ы / и are diagnosed only by tap items (A-20, B-13), never by speech; a confidently heard minimal-pair partner simply gives "let's say it together" and the model. The **quiet fallback:** if more than 60% of say-it turns in a visit come out unsure, the rest of that visit's say-it steps run as echo with an "I said it" tap, with no message.
- **Evidence:** confident = one success for a speaking (SPK) skill, representation `embodied`, capped at 50% of that skill's success evidence; the rest comes from comprehension taps, bubble choices or the parent's weekly confirmation (a "did you hear …?" card with the clip; one success per confirmation, same cap). Unsure gives **no evidence** of any kind. **Reading aloud gives no mastery evidence** (D22 applies to Russian): reading is scored by "read, then tap the picture".
- **With the microphone off:** the voice says the word, a pause for the child, the voice says it again; the child or a parent may tap "I said it" (practice only). SPK skills are then fed by comprehension taps, bubble choices and the parent's confirmation; one with none of these stays **"practised"** (the parent sees "practised", never "not mastered").
- **Release gate:** the Russian test set is every say-it word and its foils (`band-a-content.md` §A-15 and the say-it columns), read by adults on the team and, live and supervised by the owner, by children; only counts are kept. At most 1 in 20 foil readings may come out confident, and most correct readings must. If it fails, Russian say-it ships in echo mode until a better model passes. The model is chosen by the app-developer with the security-engineer (`docs/languages/speech-recognition.md` §1.3) and needs the owner's OK.

## 9. Mastery

The "Mastery" column adds what the representations must be. Core skills (marked ●) need 6 correct and 2 checks. 2-choice items count at most half of the evidence.

## 12. What is out of scope, and why
- **Cursive** (прописи): not in this plan (research report §3.5; D13).
- **Explicit case tables, verb aspect, verbs of motion as a system**: too abstract for 3–8; met only as chunks.
- **Judging speech sound by sound**: speech is judged per word only, confident or unsure (§8, D1); sounds, stress and accent are never judged from the microphone.
- **Russian culture, history or holidays beyond everyday child life and folk tales**: the role's boundary.
- **Translation exercises** Hebrew ↔ Russian: meaning by pictures instead.
