# M9: Content and the family release (about 95 skills, the missing templates, voices, art, TestFlight and Android)

**Status:** Plan written on 2026-10-28, **waiting for the owner's answers to Q1 to Q12**. Nothing is coded. M8 is merged.

This plan follows PLAN.md (the build-plan row for M9; Content & pedagogy) and `docs/curriculum.md` (sections 4, 6, 13 and 15). Where it differs, PLAN.md is updated in the same commit, as always.

## 1. Goal and "done when"

The app stops being a prototype with five skills and becomes something the children can use every day: enough lessons to last months, in all six lands, in the voices of their friends, with real art, on the family's phones and the iPad.

PLAN's acceptance for M9: **every activity passes the headless playthrough, and the release gate checklist is complete.** In plain words, the criteria I will report against:

1. **About 95 skills** (Bands A and B of Numbers, Colours, Shapes, Logic and Computer science, plus the core of Money and Maths) with about 300 activities and about 2,400 items, all through the validator.
2. **12 templates** playable by tap, drag and (where it makes sense) voice. Today there are 3.
3. **Every activity passes a headless playthrough**: a simulated child plays each activity in all its variants (right answers, each wrong answer, every hint level, "show me") and nothing crashes, dead-ends or leaves the child without a next step.
4. **Hebrew reviewed**: every child-facing string and line has `review: approved` from a person (or is listed as open in the report).
5. **Voices**: every line of the release has a recording for every character that is shipped with that voice (Q5).
6. **Art**: every skill, land, sticker and reward has its picture (real art, or a listed placeholder).
7. **Release gate checklist** (section 6) complete, and the app installed and working on the owner's Samsung phone, his wife's phone and the iPad.

## 2. How we verify

As before: everything that can be proved without a child is proved by tests first (the validator over every file, the simulator, the headless playthrough, goldens in right to left with the real fonts, the Deno and pgTAP suites), then phone checks by the owner, then **child tests** (sections 4 and 14 of the curriculum): a template passes when at least 80 percent of children start correctly after the spoken instruction alone. The honest limits, which the report will say plainly: speech recognition for children is still measured only on adults (M5), the efficacy of the lessons is not measured by this milestone, and the child tests are the owner's own two children.

## 4. Tasks, in order

### Part 9a: the pipeline and the pilot land (Counting Hills)

**A. Production pipeline**
- A1. A skeleton generator: from `docs/curriculum.md` tables to a skill file, its misconceptions and its activity stubs (ids, prerequisites, watch-for tags, representations, the hint ladder slots).
- A2. Validator rules added to the schema checks: every distractor carries a tag; at least two distractors tagged; a hint ladder with level 2 changing the representation; no self-gender in core lines; maths tokens in left-to-right isolation; nikud present on every child string; no `<` or `>` outside maths tokens.
- A3. The review states and a script that writes one review document per unit (all strings, with context) and reads the edits back.
- A4. The headless playthrough (a simulated child that plays every activity in every variant through the real lesson controller and the real templates, no UI) and a coverage report: which activities, hints, misconceptions and classifiers were reached.

**B. Placement** (Q10), small.

**C. The pilot land: Counting Hills.** The 14 Band A number skills (NUM-A-01 to A-14), about 45 activities, about 350 items, with the 4 templates they need: the three existing ones and **T14 ten-frame flash**. Hebrew strings and lines with nikud; every skill has its `childLine` and `worldObject`.

**D. The pilot's voices and art**: its lines voiced for the flagship voices (typed OK on count and cost first), its drawings from the art batch or placeholders.

**E. Report on 9a** (what the pipeline costs per skill: my time, the review time, the voice cost), so Q4 and Q5 are decided with numbers.

### Part 9b: the templates (Q6, in the order given)
- One template at a time, each with: the Flutter session class, the engine adapter (error classifiers to tags), the voice accept-list, the simulator model, goldens in right to left on three screen sizes, the playthrough, and a skill or two to prove it on.

### Part 9c: the other lands
- Colour Bay (COL), Shape Forest (SHP), Riddle Grotto (LOG), Robot Harbour (CS), Market Village (MATH), unit by unit in the curriculum's priority order; each unit ends with the owner's review of the Hebrew and a phone check.

### Part 9d: voices and art for everything
- The voice runs, in batches with typed counts and costs, with the `cheer` style for celebrations; the art batches as they arrive; the sticker set grows with the lessons.

### Part 9e: the release
- **The release gate checklist** (section 6), the family builds: Android release signing (the debug key is used today), the Android install on both phones, the iOS gate (Q9) and TestFlight, the privacy text, the owner's backup of every key, the server limits re-checked, and a final full playthrough on the real packs.

## 8. Security and child-safety controls this milestone touches

| Control | How M9 keeps it |
|---|---|
| Model output is untrusted; prompts built on the server from ids | New items and misconceptions are published to the server as before (`content-publish`); the tutor's lexicon is extended with the new vocabulary and the ten checks run over it; a test refuses a pack whose words the lexicon lacks |
| Nikud never generated by a model at runtime | All Hebrew is authored in files and reviewed; the validator refuses unpointed child text |
| No child audio stored or sent; the mic only on tap | Unchanged; the new templates take voice only through the existing matcher |
| No ads, purchases, timers, streaks or guilt | Source rules extended over the new templates (no countdowns in play; a speed-based challenge is never a template) |
| Original characters only; nothing franchise | The art brief and the review checklist say so; the owner approves each batch |
| Money and cost | Every paid generation shows the count and cost and waits for the owner's typed OK; mocks in tests |
| Network hosts only from `app/network_hosts.json` | Unchanged; the packs are bundled or installed from files, no new host |

## 10. Risks and unknowns

- **The size.** About 300 activities is a lot of content for one author. The five parts and the Band A first release (Q4) are how the risk is kept small; the 9a report gives the real cost per skill before anything else is committed.
- **Hebrew quality.** I write fluent Hebrew with nikud, but a person must read it before it is called approved; some of it will need correction, and that review time is the real bottleneck.
- **Voice cost and quota** (Q5): 1.35 million characters needs a plan above Creator; with flagship voices only, one month of Creator is enough.
- **Child safety of generated content** is proved by rules, not by me alone: the validator and the review states are the safety net.
- **Placeholder art** will look like a prototype until the illustrator delivers; the brief makes every piece replaceable.
- **Speech recognition for children** is still unmeasured; the release hides voice answers where the recogniser is not proved.
- **iOS** may uncover work (App Attest, review rules) that nothing in this plan can estimate; it is last on purpose.
