## 1. Goal and "done when"

From PLAN.md, unchanged:

- Content publishing to the server tables; the lexicon with nikud; `tutor-turn` with the validator and voice in one call; caps, kill switches and the tutor log.
- **Done when:** every gateway security test passes; median time to tutor voice is 1.5 s or less; offline and failure paths fall back to RuleTutor.
- **The owner's tightening (2026-10-26):** an answer must feel immediate. The PLAN figure of 1.5 s is the ceiling for a line that has to be made on the spot; M6 aims much lower for the lines a child actually meets, by making most of them **before** the child needs them (section 2a).

M6 gives the guide a mind, carefully. When a child's answer is unexpected, the phone asks the server for one short Socratic sentence. The server builds the prompt itself from its own tables, asks Claude Haiku 5.5, checks the reply with ten hard checks, makes its voice with ElevenLabs in the guide's own voice, and returns both in one call. Anything that goes wrong (offline, slow, refused, over a cap, a kill switch, a failed check) falls back to the authored line the app already has: the "RuleTutor". The AI is **off by default** and nothing in the lessons depends on it.

What M6 does **not** do: the parent controls for the AI, the 30-day log screen, the alerts and the push (M7); family sync (`sync-push` and `sync-pull`, M7); the parent's "Import character" and `character-register` (M7); hosting packs and the speech model on R2 (see Q1); the full 3,000-word lexicon (see Q3); iOS (the iOS gate comes later).

## 2. How we verify

Everything that can be proved without money or keys is proved by tests: the Deno suite (already run in CI), the real-database integration test, the pgTAP suite, and a new **evaluation suite** of 300 scripted scenarios that runs the validator over good and bad replies (the PLAN target: good replies wrongly rejected at most 10%). The model and ElevenLabs are replaced by fakes in tests, so a test knows exactly what was asked of them and can make them fail, be slow, or lie. The **live proof** (a real Anthropic call, a real ElevenLabs call, the real latency) needs your keys and your go-ahead on the cost, so it is a separate, owner-gated phase (Phase F), like M4's real voices.

## 2a. Making it feel immediate

**Where the time goes in a live turn** (my estimates, to be replaced by measurements in Phase F): the phone to Frankfurt and back about 100 to 150 ms; the four checks and the caps today about 150 ms; the model 0.5 to 1.0 s (Hebrew takes many tokens, and Haiku must write about 60 of them); the voice 0.3 to 0.6 s for a short line; playback start 50 ms. That is 1.2 to 2 s. A faster model or voice only trims it. The real saving is to **not wait at all** for most lines. Seven measures, in order of effect:

1. **Make the line before it is needed (prefetch).** When an activity opens, and the AI helper is on, the phone asks the server for the lines for the one or two mistakes that activity can produce (the options that carry a misconception, which the content already knows). The answers wait in memory. When the child makes that mistake, the line plays at once: **no network wait at all**. This is the biggest effect, and it needs no change to what the AI does or how it sounds.
2. **Remember what was already made (a shared cache on the server).** A line is determined by the item, the kind of mistake, the hint level, the band, the address form and the guide. Once one has passed the validator, it is stored with its audio and served to every later request in about 100 to 200 ms server time, with **no model call and no voice cost**. The same child, a sibling or tomorrow's session all benefit. Only lines that passed the ten checks are stored; the cache is a log of safe lines, never of raw model output.
3. **Warm it up in advance (pre-warm).** A tool on your PC runs the starter pack's items, mistakes, bands, address forms and guides through the full pipeline once, so even the first child sees cache hits. It shows the count of lines and characters and the cost first, and waits for your OK (like M4's voices).
4. **Cover the wait with something true and immediate.** The phone already shows the gentle wobble and the guide's "thinking" pose within 100 ms, and the lesson already pauses a second before the child may try again. The request **starts at the moment of the answer**, not after that pause, so that second is not extra time, and the authored "not yet" line plays at once. The child hears a real reaction instantly, and the tutor's sentence follows.
5. **Trim the server.** One database round trip does the device check, the caps and the config read together (instead of several); the token keys are cached; the function is kept warm by the `app-config` call at the start of a lesson; the prompt's fixed part uses Anthropic's prompt caching (lower time to first token, lower cost); the model's reply format is minimal (short keys, no extra fields), so it writes fewer tokens; the model call starts as soon as the body passes its schema, in parallel with logging.
6. **Pick the voice for speed as well as quality.** The audition tool of M4 will also print each candidate model's **time to first audio** for the same line, so you choose with your ears and the stopwatch together. Short lines (Band A is one sentence of at most ten words) keep the voice call near the low end.
7. **Never speak what has not been checked.** What I will *not* do to save time is stream the model's words straight into the voice: the ten checks need the whole line, and PLAN makes them the hard gate. A faster model that fails the checks more often would be slower overall; Haiku 5.5 stays.

**What this should give** (targets, measured in Phase F and reported as measured):

| Case | Share of turns (expected) | Time from the mistake to the tutor's voice starting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prefetched in the phone's memory | most turns once the app has run for a while | **under 100 ms** (the line is already there) |
| Server cache hit | the next most common | about **0.3 to 0.4 s** at the phone |
| Made live (new item, odd mistake, cache cold) | rare after pre-warming | **median 1.5 s or less, 95th percentile under 2.5 s**; the authored line at 3 s remains the last resort |

The PLAN's figure (median 1.5 s) is therefore the worst case, not the typical one. When the report says "median", it will give the number for **all** turns of a scripted session and separately for each of the three cases, with the share of each.

**Cost of speed.** Prefetching makes some lines nobody uses. They are counted against their own cap (prefetch turns, default 40 per session), count toward the family's daily cost cap only when they miss the cache, and a cache hit costs nothing, so the extra spend is small and bounded; the report will quote the real figure.

## 4. Tasks, in order

### Phase A — Database and content publishing

- **A1.** Migration: `app.content_item`, `app.misconception`, `app.family_character`, `app.tutor_log`, `app.tutor_cache`, with no grants for `anon` or `authenticated`, RLS on, a policy for the gateway role only; retention (30 days) for `tutor_log`; pgTAP checks added to the suite (closed schema, no direct access, constraints).
- **A2.** `content-publish` function: callable only with the publish secret (a separate check, no device), strict schema, idempotent upsert by pack id and version, refuses a lower version; rows hold the prompt text, answer forms, number set, allowed actions, hint threshold and object ids, but no audio.
- **A3.** `pack_builder publish <pack> [--dry-run]`: reads a **verified** pack, builds the item index from its documents (activities, misconceptions, characters), shows what it would send (counts, no secret), and posts it. The secret is read from an environment variable only. Tests against a fake server.

### Phase B — Hebrew text and the lexicon (TypeScript)

- **B1.** `hebrew.ts`: normalise, prefix-aware lookup, the lexicon loader, the gender and address-form tables for verbs, number words, the vocaliser; `testvectors/hebrew.json` run by both Dart and TS.
- **B2.** The starter lexicon `content/lexicon/he-child.v1.json` (about 400 words, vocalised, `needs-editor`) and a checker that fails on a form with no reading, with two readings, with a Latin letter, or that appears in the blocklists.

### Phase C — The validator and the evaluation suite

- **C1.** The ten checks, in the order of PLAN: schema, characters, length by band, gender, answer leak, numbers, vocabulary, pronunciation, blocklists, targets. Each returns a fixed reason code.
- **C2.** `TutorAction` schema (shared by server and app) and the RuleTutor action ids.
- **C3.** The 300-scenario evaluation: good and bad replies per rule, the pass rate per rule, the 10% good-reply rejection target, run in CI.

### Phase D — `tutor-turn`

- **D1.** The function on the existing handler: the four checks, strict body (ids, numbers and enums only; every id must exist for the named pack version), kill switches and caps with one atomic counter update, a clean refusal code for each.
- **D2.** The prompt builder: a fixed, versioned template filled only from the server's tables; the model id pinned to `claude-haiku-5-5`; 300 tokens; timeouts 1.6 s for the model and 0.9 s for voice; a retry only if at least 1.5 s remain; the injected clients for Anthropic and ElevenLabs.
- **D2a.** One database round trip for the device check, the caps and the config; the cache lookup first (a hit returns before any model or voice work); the prompt's fixed part uses prompt caching; the model's reply format is minimal.
- **D2b.** `tutor-turn` accepts a **prefetch** request (up to three contexts at once, no audio returned unless asked), fills the cache through the same validator, and has its own cap.
- **D3.** Voice in the same call: only for a line that just passed, with the voice id from `family_character`; the history item deleted after use; the line, its audio (base64) and the vocalised caption (for Bands B and C) returned.
- **D4.** `tutor_log` on every outcome (line, validator result, request ids), and logs that carry only a request id, function, latency and outcome.
- **D5.** Security tests for the new functions (section 7), including a source rule that forbids any key or child word in a log call.

### Phase E — The app side

- **E1.** `TutorPolicy` with `RuleTutor` (today's authored feedback, now behind the interface) and `LlmTutor` (the server call); `TutorClient` over the existing `GatewayClient`; the context is ids, numbers and enums only, built from the lesson.
- **E1a.** **Prefetch:** when an activity opens, ask for the lines of its likely mistakes; keep them in memory for the activity; a hit plays with no network wait. The request for a missed line starts **at the moment of the answer**, in parallel with the authored acknowledgement and the existing 1-second pause.
- **E2.** The 3-second plan: "thinking" within 100 ms (the guide's thinking pose), the recorded "hmm…" line at 1.5 s (a core line already voiced), the authored line at 3.0 s; the circuit breaker (3 failures end AI for the session); the kill switches and minimum version from `app-config`, cached 15 minutes; a light `app-config` call when a lesson starts to warm the function.
- **E3.** Playing a live line: the director plays the returned bytes in the guide's voice with a plain mouth flap (live lines have no cue file); the caption by band; one new core line is the only text the app adds itself.
- **E4.** The dev-page switch "AI helper" (default off), and the trigger of Q4 in the lesson controller.
- **E5.** Widget tests with a fake gateway for every path: success, offline, slow, refused, over cap, kill switch, validator failure, circuit breaker; and a test that nothing but ids and numbers is ever sent.

## 9. Risks and unknowns

- **The lexicon is small at first,** so the validator will reject many real replies and the AI will be quiet. That is the safe direction; the 10% target is measured against the starter lexicon's own vocabulary and the report says so.
- **Hebrew inflection is hard.** The gender check relies on tables I write for the lexicon's verbs; forms ambiguous without nikud are ignored by design. A wrong table entry would let a wrong form through or reject a right one: the shared vectors and the reviewed lexicon are the guard, and a Hebrew editor is the real fix.
- **Prefetch spends a little on lines nobody uses,** and a prefetched line can go stale if the child answers differently from the prediction (then the live path runs, as before). Capped and measured.
- **The cache could serve an out-of-date line** after a prompt, validator or content change: the key includes their versions, and a test fails if a version is not part of the key.
- **1.5 s may not be reachable for a line made live.** A cold Edge Function, the model (1.6 s budget) and the voice (0.9 s) together can exceed 1.5 s; the plan's own budget is 3 s end to end. The report will give the real median and the fraction under 1.5 s and 3 s, not a claim.
- **The model may be slow or refuse to follow the JSON format.** The timeout and the schema check make that a fallback, not a failure; a poor first-time pass rate would show in the live evaluation, and the prompt is versioned so it can be improved.
- **Provider limits and prices change.** I will look up the current Haiku and ElevenLabs prices on their pages before any live run and show the arithmetic.
- **ElevenLabs history deletion.** If the header or endpoint behaves differently from the docs, the request id is still logged and the sweep job (M7) catches it.
- **Supabase free plan.** `pg_cron` pauses with the project; the 30-day retention then catches up on resume (PLAN already notes this).
- **Stacked branches.** M6 sits on M5, which sits on M4, neither merged. A phone problem in M4 or M5 means rework there first.

The 3,000-word lexicon grows with the content (M9).

### Teaching moves and reviewed lines (2026-10-27)

Why the lines were samey: the model was told about a mistake only through the parent-facing text ("the child misses an object"), never what to teach. `content/tutor-moves.json` now gives each misconception up to three help levels with two to four ways to help at each (English, authored, `review: needs-educator`); one is picked by band and address form, so cells differ and a cell is stable. The first run with moves loaded: **75 of 99 lines pass (76%), 66 different sentences among the 75** (before: 95 of 99, 57 among 95): skipped and double-counted mistakes now get different help, at the price of more refusals while the lexicon (now 570 words) catches up.

The checks guarantee safety and the lexicon's words, not good Hebrew: of the 66 sentences about 8 were ungrammatical or wrong ("ונעבור לפרח הבא" for butterflies, "בוא נוסיף מצבע את כל הפרפרים"). So a person reads the lines before a child hears them (the review and voice flow, PLAN section on reviewed lines). Cost of the voice flow: voice only, no model.

**Reviewed lines voiced (2026-10-27).** The owner struck 9 of the 66 sentences (broken grammar, "flower" for "butterfly", nonsense); `tutor_prewarm voice` then voiced the other 57 sentences for 66 cells: 0 refused by the re-run checks, 0.18 USD (estimate 0.22), 17.7 KB of audio per line on average, kept under the prompt version `approved`. Total spent on the live phase so far: about 0.29 (first pre-warm) + 0.18 (voice) + six model-only evaluations at 0.04 to 0.05 = roughly 0.75 USD. **Known nits for the Hebrew editor (R1), all from the lexicon's prefix rule, not from the model:** a prefix before a word that starts with a dagesh gets a second one (בְּפַּרְפָּר, לְכָּל), and "בְּיַחַד" comes out as בַּיַחַד. They change the sound very little but should be fixed in `vocalisePrefix` or by listing the prefixed forms.
