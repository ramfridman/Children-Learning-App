# Curriculum research for a Nitznutz subject

The owner's standard: every subject is taught "properly" - correct, based on real and working teaching techniques, and right for the child's age and level. This skill is how a teacher agent gets there and shows it.

## 1. Frame the question
Write down, before searching: the subject, the bands (A 3-4, B 5-6, C 7-8; `docs/PLAN.md` › Learners & age bands), the learners' first language (Hebrew), what the app can do (tap, drag, trace, listen, a parent-enabled mic on a tap; no timers or streaks), and the decision the research must support.

## 2. Sources, in this order of weight
1. **The official programme**: the Israeli Ministry of Education programme for the subject and age (kindergarten and grades 1-3), in its current version, read from the Ministry's own site (pop.education.gov.il, meyda.education.gov.il). For a language not taught in Israeli schools at this age, the programme of the country where it is a school subject, plus heritage-language programmes if relevant.
2. **Research syntheses**: meta-analyses, systematic reviews, national panel reports (for example the US National Reading Panel, the National Early Literacy Panel, the EEF toolkits, What Works Clearinghouse practice guides), and handbooks of the field.
3. **Primary studies** with children of the same ages, ideally learning in Hebrew or the target language.
4. **Frameworks as yardsticks**: CEFR Pre-A1 and Cambridge Young Learners for English; CSTA K-2 for computing; NCTM and learning trajectories for maths.
5. **Practice sources** (teacher guides, well-reviewed apps and programmes) only to illustrate, never as evidence.

Rules: cite every claim with author, year, title and a link; **never invent a citation, a page or a number**; when a source cannot be opened, say so; when the evidence is thin, mixed or from older children, say that plainly and say what you chose and why.

## 3. Map it
- A skill list per band, each with: an id in the land's pattern, the can-do statement a parent understands, prerequisites (no cycles), the programme reference, the evidence reference, and how the app will see mastery.
- Misconceptions and typical errors per skill, each with the signal that reveals it and the re-teach that repairs it.
- The representations or materials that carry each idea, and what would mislead.
- What is out of scope for the app and why.

## 4. Write it
- The research report: `docs/research/<subject>-<topic>-<yyyy-mm-dd>.md`: the question, the sources read (with links), what they agree on, where they disagree, the decision, and the open questions for the owner.
- The syllabus: `docs/languages/<language>/syllabus.md` for a language, `docs/curriculum.md` (with the curriculum-expert) for the other subjects.
- New child-facing text is a draft: Hebrew marked `"review": "needs-editor"`; English or Russian reviewed by its teacher agent before it is final.

## 5. Hand back
The report names the files written, the sources relied on, every decision the owner must make (numbered, with a recommendation), and the hand-offs (curriculum-expert for the shared engine, design-director for pictures, voice-and-sound-director for voices, app-developer for templates).
